# Trust Compliance: Missouri

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Missouri imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Missouri nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the settlor was a Missouri resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Missouri-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Missouri-source income generally have no Missouri income tax filing obligation.

Missouri has a graduated income tax rate structure for trusts, with rates ranging from 0% to 4.70% for the 2025 tax year. The Missouri taxable income is calculated on Form MO-1041, the Missouri Fiduciary Income Tax Return. Missouri follows the federal taxable income as a starting point and applies Missouri-specific modifications.

## Filing Requirements

Missouri fiduciary income tax is filed using Form MO-1041, the Missouri Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is the 15th day of the fourth month following the close of the calendar or fiscal year. Form MO-1041 is due annually. Every resident estate or trust that is required to file a Federal Form 1041 must also file a Missouri return. Nonresident estates or trusts with any taxable income from sources within Missouri or gross income of $1,000 or more must also file. Missouri does not require estimated tax payments for estates or trusts.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Missouri does not impose a mandatory statutory beneficiary notice requirement under the Missouri Trust Code (Mo. Rev. Stat. Chapter 456). Trustees are not required by statute to send a specific notice to beneficiaries upon acceptance, though the trust instrument may impose such obligations. Missouri follows the Uniform Trust Code structure, and trustees are presumed to have fulfilled their duty if they comply with the notice and information requirements prescribed in the trust instrument.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Missouri is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise. Missouri probate courts have jurisdiction over trust proceedings, and courts can require, dispense with, or modify or terminate a bond, and adjust trustee compensation.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Spendthrift Trusts:** Missouri allows spendthrift trusts under Mo. Rev. Stat. Section 456.5-502. A term providing that the interest of a beneficiary is held subject to a "spendthrift trust" is sufficient to restrain both voluntary and involuntary transfers.
- **No DAPT:** Missouri does not allow self-settled asset protection trusts. A settlor cannot place assets in a trust for their own benefit and shield those assets from creditors.
- **Directed Trusts:** Missouri has adopted provisions for directed trusts and nonjudicial settlement agreements, giving trustees and beneficiaries more flexibility in trust administration.
