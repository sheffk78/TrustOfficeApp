# Trust Compliance: Mississippi

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Mississippi imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Mississippi nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was a Mississippi resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Mississippi-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Mississippi-source income generally have no Mississippi income tax filing obligation.

Mississippi has a graduated income tax rate structure for trusts, with rates of 0% on the first $10,000 of taxable income and 4% on income exceeding $10,000 for the 2025 tax year. Mississippi generally follows IRS rules concerning the calculation of taxable income. The fiduciary income tax return is filed using Form 81-100, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return.

## Filing Requirements

Mississippi fiduciary income tax is filed using Form 81-100, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form 81-100 is due annually. The fiduciary or an officer must provide their name and signature. If the income tax is not paid by the original due date of the return, interest is due at the rate of 1/2% per month. Mississippi follows federal rules for determining taxable income, and the trust must file if it has Mississippi-source income.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Mississippi does not impose a mandatory statutory beneficiary notice requirement under the Mississippi Uniform Trust Code (Miss. Code Ann. Title 91, Chapter 8). Trustees are not required by statute to send a specific notice to beneficiaries upon acceptance, though the trust instrument may impose such obligations. Under Section 91-8-813, trustees have duties to keep beneficiaries informed and to respond to requests for information, but these duties may be waived or modified by the settlor in the trust instrument.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Mississippi is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise. Mississippi courts have authority over trust matters under Title 91, Chapter 8 of the Mississippi Code, including the power to require, dispense with, or modify or terminate a bond, and to adjust trustee compensation.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Spendthrift Trusts:** Mississippi allows spendthrift trusts under Miss. Code Ann. Section 91-8-502. A term providing that the interest of a beneficiary is held subject to a spendthrift provision is sufficient to restrain both voluntary and involuntary transfers.
- **No DAPT:** Mississippi does not allow self-settled asset protection trusts. A settlor cannot place assets in a trust for their own benefit and shield those assets from creditors.
- **Special Needs Trusts:** Mississippi has strong protective statutes for special needs trusts. Section 91-8-1109 creates a legal firewall that prevents other provisions of Mississippi trust law from being used to disqualify a beneficiary from government benefits.
