## Trust Tax Calendar

A practical deadline tracker for trustees handling Form 1041, K-1s, estimated taxes, records, and annual
beneficiary communication.
### How to use this calendar
Use this as a working checklist. Add exact dates to your calendar, assign the CPA/accounting owner, and store every filed
return, K-1, notice, extension, and payment confirmation in the trust records. TrustOffice can help you track the tasks; your
CPA or tax attorney should confirm the legal/tax answer for your specific trust.
Key assumption: this checklist uses the common calendar-year trust pattern. Some trusts use a fiscal year, and some state
deadlines differ. Verify before relying on any deadline.

Trustee action
Why it matters
January
Confirm prior-year income, distributions, CPA engagement, beneficiary contact details, and missing records.
Avoids a March/April scramble and keeps K-1 delivery clean.
January–February
Gather brokerage statements, bank statements, income records, deductible expenses, trustee fees, legal/CPA invoices, and distribution records.
Form 1041 accuracy depends on complete records.
February
Confirm whether income was retained by the trust or distributed/passed through to beneficiaries.
Determines whether the trust pays tax or beneficiaries report income via K-1.
March
Review draft Form 1041 and Schedule K-1s with CPA. Confirm beneficiary names, SSNs/EINs, addresses, and allocation percentages.
Prevents corrected filings and beneficiary confusion.
April 15
Calendar-year Form 1041 due date and common first estimated tax payment date. File extension if needed.
Missing the date can create penalties and angry beneficiaries.
April–May
Send K-1s and beneficiary explanation. Store proof of delivery and copies in the trust records.
Beneficiaries need K-1s for personal returns. Documentation protects the trustee.
June 15
Second estimated tax checkpoint/payment if required.
Keeps the trust from underpayment penalties.
September 15
Third estimated tax checkpoint/payment; also common extended trust return deadline checkpoint.
Good time to confirm year-to-date distributions and retained income.
October
If an extension was filed, confirm final return status and all K-1 delivery is complete.
Extension is not a payment extension. Confirm no loose ends.
December
Meet with CPA/advisor before year-end distributions. Decide whether to distribute or retain income.
Year-end decisions can materially affect who pays tax and at what rate.
Ongoing
Log distributions, tax notices, CPA correspondence, and document uploads as they happen.
Tax compliance is much easier when records are contemporaneous.
