# Trust Compliance: Maine

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Maine imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Maine nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was a Maine resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Maine-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Maine-source income generally have no Maine income tax filing obligation.

Maine has a progressive income tax rate structure for trusts, with rates of 5.8%, 6.75%, and 7.15% for tax years beginning after December 31, 2015. The fiduciary or trustee of a trust must file Maine Form 1041ME if the estate or trust has any Maine-source income or if the trust is a resident trust. The tax rates are applied to the trust's Maine taxable income after applicable adjustments.

## Filing Requirements

Maine fiduciary income tax is filed using Form 1041ME, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form 1041ME is due annually. The fiduciary or trustee must file if the estate or trust has any Maine-source income or if the trust is a resident trust. Estimated tax payments may be required for trusts expecting to owe tax.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Under the Maine Uniform Trust Code (18-B M.R.S.A.), 18-B M.R.S. Section 813 is the operative beneficiary-notice provision. Within 60 days after accepting a trusteeship, the trustee must notify the qualified beneficiaries of the acceptance and of the trustee's name, address, and telephone number. Section 813 also requires the trustee to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed about the administration of the trust, to promptly respond to reasonable requests for information, and to send a written report at least annually. The settlor may waive or modify these duties by express provision in the trust instrument.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Maine is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise. Maine probate courts have jurisdiction over trust proceedings under 18-B M.R.S.A.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Asset Protection:** Maine allows spendthrift trusts under 18-B M.R.S.A. Chapter 5. A spendthrift provision restrains both voluntary and involuntary transfers of a beneficiary's interest.
- **Trustee Duties:** Maine's Trust Code (18-B M.R.S.A.) imposes detailed duties on trustees, including the duty of loyalty, the duty of impartiality, and the duty to act in the interests of the beneficiaries.
- **Waiver of Notice:** The settlor may waive or modify the notice and reporting duties of a trustee under Section 813 by express provision in the trust instrument.
