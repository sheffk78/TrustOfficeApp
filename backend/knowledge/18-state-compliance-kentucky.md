# Trust Compliance: Kentucky

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Kentucky imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Kentucky nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the settlor was a Kentucky resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Kentucky-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Kentucky-source income generally have no Kentucky income tax filing obligation.

Kentucky's income tax rate for trusts is 4% on the portion of income not distributable to beneficiaries. The tax is calculated using the individual income tax rate under KRS 141.020, which applies to estates and trusts. Kentucky's income tax law is based on the Internal Revenue Code in effect as of December 31, 2024.

## Filing Requirements

Kentucky fiduciary income tax is filed using Form 741, the Kentucky Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is the 15th day of the fourth month following the close of the tax year. Form 741 is due annually. The fiduciary of a resident estate or trust must file if the estate or trust is required to file a federal Form 1041. Nonresident estates and trusts are subject to tax on income from Kentucky sources, including activities carried on in Kentucky, performance of services in Kentucky, and real or tangible property located in Kentucky.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Kentucky does not impose a mandatory statutory beneficiary notice requirement under the Kentucky Uniform Trust Code (KRS Chapter 386B). Trustees are not required by statute to send a specific notice to beneficiaries upon acceptance, though the trust instrument may impose such obligations. KRS 386B.8-130 requires trustees to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed about the administration of the trust and of material facts necessary to protect their interests, but does not specify a particular notice deadline.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Kentucky is optional. A trust may be registered with the court under KRS 386B.2-050, but registration is not required for the trust to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise. Registered trusts are subject to the jurisdiction of the court of registration for proceedings under the Kentucky Uniform Trust Code.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Asset Protection:** Kentucky allows spendthrift trusts under KRS 386B.5-020. A spendthrift provision restrains both voluntary and involuntary transfers of a beneficiary's interest. No specific language is necessary to create a spendthrift trust.
- **Spendthrift Trusts:** Kentucky's spendthrift provisions protect the beneficiary's interest from creditors, but the protection does not extend to claims of the settlor's creditors during the settlor's lifetime for revocable trusts.
- **Trust Registration:** Kentucky allows voluntary registration of trusts with the court (KRS 386B.2-050), which can provide additional protections and streamline beneficiary disputes.
