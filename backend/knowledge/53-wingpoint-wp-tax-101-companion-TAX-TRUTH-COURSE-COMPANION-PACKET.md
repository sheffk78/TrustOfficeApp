## Documents in the Packet

### 1. Statutory Definitions Quick Reference

**One page, front and back.** The definitions that don't mean what people think they mean.

**Format:** Two-column — "What You Assume It Means" vs. "What the Code Actually Says"

**Definitions to include:**
| Term | Code Reference | Common Assumption | Statutory Reality |
|------|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Trade or Business | 26 USC 7701(a)(26) | Any work you do for money | The performance of the functions of a public office or employment under federal government |
| Employee | 26 USC 3401(c) | Anyone who works for someone | Specifically defined category with narrow application |
| Wages | 26 USC 3401(a) | All money you earn from a job | Remuneration for services by an "employee" for an "employer" — with 23 enumerated exceptions |
| Taxpayer | 26 USC 7701(a)(14) | Anyone who pays taxes | A person subject to any internal revenue tax — a legal status, not a universal condition |
| Employer | 26 USC 3401(d) | Anyone who pays someone for work | Narrowly defined under IRC — not the same as common law employer |
| U.S. Person | 26 USC 7701(a)(30) | Anyone in America | Specifically enumerated: citizens, residents, domestic entities, etc. |
| Foreign Person | 26 USC 7701(a)(30) | Someone from another country | Any person or entity NOT a U.S. person under the statute |
| Direct Tax | Constitution Art. I §9 | — | Capitation or tax on property — must be apportioned |
| Excise Tax | Constitution Art. I §8 | — | Tax on privileged activities — no apportionment required |

**Design notes:**
- Clean, printable. No fancy graphics — this is a reference card, not marketing.
- Each definition includes the exact code citation so members can verify themselves.
- Footer: "Verify everything. Read the code. This is education, not advice."

**Source to draft from:** Training knowledge modules 01, 02, 04 + GLOSSARY.md

### 2. W-4 Decision Guide

**Two pages.** What the form actually is, what each line means, what you're certifying, and what the code says about each status.

**Sections:**
- **What is a W-4?** — A withholding allowance certificate under §3402(f). Not a contract. Not a confession. A form that tells your employer how much to withhold based on what you claim.
- **What you're certifying when you sign** — The language on the form and what it means in plain English
- **Line by line: what each field actually asks** — Filing status, allowances, additional withholding, exemption claim
- **What the code says about exemption** — §3402(n): no withholding required if the employee had no income tax liability last year and expects none this year
- **The difference between informed and uninformed completion** — Most people fill it out because HR told them to. What changes when you understand what you're signing?
- **Important framing:** This guide teaches you what the form is and what the code says. What you do with that information is your decision.

**Design notes:**
- No sample filled-out forms (violates the "no templates" boundary)
- No "fill it out this way" language
- Questions, not instructions: "What does this line mean?" not "Check this box"

**Source to draft from:** Training `02-withholding-employee/irc-withholding-statutes-20260424.md` + Lost Horizons W-4 content ⚠️ **GAP — need to add W-4 specific guidance to knowledge base**

### 3. Corrected Filing Checklist

**Two pages.** The procedural map from Module 7 — what forms exist, what they're for, and the general sequence. Not specific instructions.

**Sections:**
- **When a correction may be appropriate** — Information on your reporting documents doesn't match your understanding of the code
- **Form 4852 — Substitute W-2** — What it is, when it's used, what information it requires, where to file it
- **Corrected W-2 process** — The general sequence for disputing the characterization on a W-2 you received
- **1099 corrections** — Same principle, different form type. What a corrected 1099 means and the general process
- **K-1 corrections** — Trust and partnership income reporting. How it interacts with your filing position.
- **Supplemental returns** — What they are, when they're used, how they differ from amended returns
- **The transition sequence** — Not overnight. One step at a time. Documentation and consistency matter more than speed.
- **General principles:** (1) Verify before you act. (2) Document everything. (3) Consistency over speed. (4) This is education about process, not specific filing advice.

**Design notes:**
- Checklist format — each section has a "understand this before proceeding" box
- No pre-filled forms or templates
- Footer: "This is procedural education, not filing advice. Consult qualified professionals for your specific situation."

### 4. Key Cases & Statutes Reference Card

**One page, dense but scannable.** The foundational legal references that support the course's arguments.

**Format:** Citation | What it established | Why it matters

**Cases to include:**
| Case | Citation | Key Holding |
|------|----------|-------------|
| Brushaber v. Union Pacific | 240 U.S. 1 (1916) | 16th Amendment did not change the constitutional requirements for direct vs. indirect taxes; income tax remains an excise |
| Pollock v. Farmers' Loan & Trust | 157 U.S. 429 (1895) | Tax on income from property is a direct tax requiring apportionment |
| Flint v. Stone Tracy Co. | 220 U.S. 107 (1911) | Excise taxes are taxes on privilege, not on property itself |
| Hodges v. United States | 203 U.S. 1 (1906) | 14th/15th Amendments operate solely on state action, not individual action; federal government remains one of enumerated powers |
| Eisner v. Macomber | 252 U.S. 189 (1920) | Income must be derived — not every receipt is income under the 16th Amendment |

**Statutes to include:**
| Statute | What it defines | Key takeaway |
|---------|-----------------|--------------|
| 26 USC §7701(a)(26) | Trade or business | Narrower than common usage — federal privilege, not private earning |
| 26 USC §3401(a) | Wages | 23 enumerated exceptions — most private compensation may be excluded |
| 26 USC §3401(c) | Employee | Statutory category, not common law concept |
| 26 USC §3402(a) | Withholding | Only applies to "wages" as defined in §3401 |
| 26 USC §3402(n) | Exemption from withholding | No withholding if no tax liability last year and none expected |
| 26 USC §508(c)(1)(a) | Mandatory exception | Churches automatically exempt — no application required |
| 26 USC §7611 | Church audit procedures | IRS has limited authority to examine churches |
| 26 USC §676 | Revocation of trust | Power to revoke treated as power of grantor |

**Design notes:**
- Laminated-card feel — this is the thing people keep in a drawer and pull out when they need a citation
- Each entry includes the exact citation for independent verification
- Footer: "Read the original sources. Verify for yourself."

**Source to draft from:** Training modules 01, 04, 08 + existing case citations throughout knowledge base

### 5. Reading Path Guide

**One page.** Ordered reading list — where to start, what comes next, what to save for later.

**Reading order:**
1. **Lost Horizons** (losthorizons.com) — Start here. The nature of tax, Bob's Bicycles, the 16th Amendment analysis
2. **Cracking the Code** (Pete Hendrickson) — The detailed filing revelation. Dense but essential.
3. **IRS Publication 1828** — The government's own words on church exemptions and audit protections. Read what they say about their own authority.
4. **Freedom Law Group White Paper** — Revocation of election. The process argument.
5. **SEDM Knowledge Base** — For those ready to go deeper: NRA position, KYC disclosure, common law practice guide
6. **Advanced (optional):** Modern Money Mechanics, Burlamaqui's Natural Law, Wise's Treatise on American Citizenship

**For each entry:**
- One-sentence description of what it covers
- Difficulty level (foundational / intermediate / advanced)
- Where to find it
- What to read first within it (specific section/chapter)

**Design notes:**
- Prevents the "I found one article and now I'm drowning" problem
- Clear difficulty ratings so people don't jump to advanced material before the foundation
- Footer: "Take it in order. The sequence matters."

**Source to draft from:** Course outline Module 9 + training knowledge base index
