# Trust Compliance: Nebraska

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Nebraska imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Nebraska nexus. A trust is considered to have Nebraska nexus if it is a resident trust â meaning the grantor was a Nebraska resident when the trust became irrevocable â or if it has Nebraska-source income such as real estate, business operations, or rental property located in the state. Nonresident trusts with no Nebraska-source income generally have no Nebraska income tax filing obligation.

Nebraska's income tax rates for trusts are graduated. The rates range from 2.46% on the first portion of taxable income up to 5.2% on higher income levels. Nebraska taxes trusts at the same graduated rate schedule applied to individual taxpayers. Fiduciaries should plan distributions carefully because Nebraska allows a deduction for income distributed to beneficiaries, which can shift the tax burden to beneficiaries who may be in lower brackets.

## Filing Requirements

Nebraska fiduciary income tax is filed using Form 1041N, the Nebraska Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form 1041N is due annually. A fiduciary must file Form 1041N if the trust is required to file a federal Form 1041 and has Nebraska-source income or Nebraska-resident beneficiaries. An extension can be filed, which provides additional time to file but not to pay.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Nebraska does not have a statutory requirement for trustees to provide initial notice to beneficiaries upon accepting trusteeship or when a trust becomes irrevocable. The Nebraska Uniform Trust Code (Nebraska Revised Statutes Â§Â§30-3801 through 30-3815) requires trustees to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed about the administration of the trust and the material facts necessary for them to protect their interests. Trustees must respond to beneficiary requests for information and provide reports. While there is no mandatory initial notice deadline, trustees should document all communications with beneficiaries to create a defensible record.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Nebraska is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise or if judicial guidance is needed. Nebraska courts can also intervene when a trustee requests instructions or when a beneficiary seeks enforcement of their rights under the trust. The Nebraska Uniform Trust Code provides that a trust is not subject to continuing judicial supervision unless ordered by the court.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Uniform Trust Code:** Nebraska adopted the UTC effective January 2, 2005, codified as Nebraska Revised Statutes Â§Â§30-3801 through 30-3815. Nebraska's adoption closely follows the model UTC with some state-specific modifications.
- **Spendthrift Protection:** Nebraska allows self-settled asset protection trusts, meaning a grantor can place assets in a trust for their own benefit and shield those assets from creditors, subject to certain limitations under the Nebraska Trust Code.
- **Perpetuity Period:** Nebraska has abolished the rule against perpetuities, allowing trusts to last indefinitely for multi-generational wealth planning.
- **Community Property:** Nebraska is not a community property state, following the equitable distribution model for property division.
