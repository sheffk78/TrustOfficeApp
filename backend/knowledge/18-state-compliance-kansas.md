# Trust Compliance: Kansas

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Kansas imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Kansas nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was a Kansas resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Kansas-source income such as real estate or business operations in the state. Nonresident trusts with no Kansas-source income generally have no Kansas income tax filing obligation.

Kansas has a progressive income tax structure for trusts, with rates ranging from 5.2% to 5.58% for the 2025 tax year. Trusts are taxed at the individual rate schedule. Fiduciaries should plan distributions carefully because Kansas allows a deduction for income distributed to beneficiaries, which can shift the tax burden to beneficiaries who may be in lower brackets.

## Filing Requirements

Kansas fiduciary income tax is filed using Form K-41, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form K-41 is due annually. The fiduciary of a resident estate or trust must file if the estate or trust had any taxable income or if withholding tax is due for nonresident beneficiaries. An extension can be filed, but the specific extension form and timeline should be confirmed with the Kansas Department of Revenue.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Kansas does not impose a mandatory statutory beneficiary notice requirement under the Kansas Uniform Trust Code (KSA Chapter 58a). Trustees are not required by statute to send a specific notice to beneficiaries upon acceptance or at other intervals, though the trust instrument may impose such obligations. Trustees should review the trust document carefully for any notice provisions that may have been included by the settlor.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Kansas is optional. A trust is not subject to continuing judicial supervision unless ordered by the court (KSA 58a-2-050). Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise. Kansas probate courts have jurisdiction over trust proceedings under KSA Chapter 58a and Chapter 59.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Asset Protection:** Kansas does not allow self-settled asset protection trusts. Kansas is not among the states that have enacted domestic asset protection trust (DAPT) legislation, so a self-settled spendthrift trust formed in Kansas provides the settlor no protection from the settlor's creditors.
- **Spendthrift Trusts:** Kansas allows spendthrift provisions under KSA 58a-502. A term providing that the beneficiary's interest is held subject to a "spendthrift trust" is sufficient to restrain both voluntary and involuntary transfers.
- **Perpetuity Period:** Kansas has adopted the statutory rule against perpetuities under KSA 59-3401. A nonvested property interest is invalid unless it is certain to vest or terminate no later than 21 years after the death of an individual then alive, or within 90 years after its creation.
