# Trust Compliance: Illinois

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Illinois imposes a state income tax on trusts, making it a significant tax jurisdiction for trust administration. A trust is subject to Illinois income tax if it is a resident trust or if it has Illinois-source income. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was an Illinois resident at the time the trust became irrevocable or, for trusts created by will, if the decedent was an Illinois resident at death.

Illinois taxes resident trusts on all income regardless of source. Nonresident trusts are taxed only on Illinois-source income. The Illinois income tax rate is a flat 4.95% for trusts, which is the same rate that applies to individuals. While the flat rate is lower than the progressive rates in states like California and New York, it still represents a meaningful tax burden, especially for trusts accumulating income.

Importantly, an Illinois resident trust is exempt from Illinois income tax if it has no Illinois-source income and no Illinois trustee. This is similar to New York's exempt resident trust rule. If the trustee is located outside Illinois and the trust holds no Illinois-situs assets, the trust may avoid Illinois income tax entirely. This requires careful structuring and ongoing compliance monitoring.

## Filing Requirements

Illinois fiduciary income tax is filed using Form IL-1041, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, aligning with the federal Form 1041 deadline. If the federal return is extended, Illinois grants an automatic extension for filing, but payment of any tax owed is still due by the original April 15 deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

Estimated tax payments are required if the trust expects to owe Illinois tax of $1,000 or more for the year. Form IL-1041-ES is used for quarterly estimated payments, due on April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15. Trustees must monitor income throughout the year to ensure estimated payments are adequate.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Illinois has beneficiary notice requirements under the Illinois Probate Act. Under Section 11-1 of the Probate Act, trustees have a duty to account to beneficiaries. For trusts created after January 1, 2010, the trustee must provide an annual accounting to beneficiaries unless the trust instrument waives this requirement or the beneficiaries have waived it in writing.

Illinois also requires that upon the death of the grantor of a revocable trust, the trustee must notify the heirs at law and the beneficiaries of the trust of the trust's existence and provide information about the trust terms. This notice should be given within a reasonable time after the grantor's death, typically 60 to 90 days. The notice must include the identity of the trustee, the address of the trustee, and information about the beneficiaries' rights to receive accountings.

## Court Supervision

Illinois trusts are generally administered without court supervision. The Illinois Trust Code, enacted in 2020 and codified in the Probate Act, allows trusts to operate independently of the court. Trustees are not required to register trusts with the court or file annual reports. However, the Probate Court has jurisdiction over trust matters, and beneficiaries or trustees can petition the court for supervision, accounting, or resolution of disputes.

For testamentary trusts (created by will), the trust may fall under the jurisdiction of the Probate Court, and the trustee may be required to file periodic accountings. Revocable living trusts and irrevocable inter vivos trusts are typically unsupervised unless a party requests court involvement.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Flat Income Tax Rate:** Illinois applies a flat 4.95% income tax to trust income, simplifying rate calculations but still representing a meaningful cost.
- **Exempt Resident Trust Rule:** Resident trusts with no Illinois-source income and no Illinois trustee may be exempt from Illinois income tax.
- **Illinois Trust Code:** Illinois modernized its trust laws in 2020, providing clearer rules on trustee duties, beneficiary rights, and trust administration.
- **Illinois Estate Tax:** Illinois imposes a separate state estate tax with an exemption amount that differs from the federal exemption (currently $4 million). Trustees and executors must evaluate Illinois estate tax filing requirements independently from federal estate tax.
