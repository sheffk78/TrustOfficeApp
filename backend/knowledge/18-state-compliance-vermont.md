# Trust Compliance: Vermont

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Vermont imposes a state income tax on trusts with Vermont nexus. A trust is a Vermont resident for tax purposes if any portion of the trust consists of property transferred by a Vermont resident at irrevocability, if a revocable trust holds property transferred by a Vermont resident, or if property was transferred by the will of a Vermont-domiciled decedent. Vermont taxes estates and trusts using the same brackets applied to individuals, with rates ranging from 3.35% up to a top rate of 8.75% on higher taxable income (see 32 V.S.A. § 5822). Because the top bracket is reached at a moderate income level, trusts with significant undistributed income can land in the upper brackets quickly.

Income distributed to beneficiaries is generally deductible at the trust level and taxable to the beneficiaries. Vermont requires estimated payments for fiduciary income tax under 32 V.S.A. § 5852, and payments are made through the myVTax portal or paper vouchers on the standard quarterly schedule.

## Filing Requirements

Vermont fiduciary income tax is filed on Form FIT-161, the Fiduciary Return of Income. A return must be filed for every estate and trust required to file a federal Form 1041, and also for any settlement fund or electing small business trust required to file a federal return, where Vermont nexus or Vermont-source income exists. The deadline for calendar-year trusts is April 15, with a September 30 extended due date if an extension is filed. Any tax due must be paid by the original deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Vermont adopted the Uniform Trust Code in 2007, and its trust statutes in Title 14A of the Vermont Statutes Annotated follow the Uniform Trust Code framework. Under the trust code, a trustee has a duty to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration and must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries when a trust becomes irrevocable or when a change of trustee occurs. Trustees should document notice delivery dates and maintain records of what information was provided, as the duty-to-inform provisions create a compliance burden for fiduciaries.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Vermont trusts is optional, and a trust does not need to be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate in an unsupervised manner. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the Superior Court for supervision or guidance when disputes arise or judicial direction is needed. Court supervision provides additional liability protection but adds cost and delay, so it is generally used only for accountings that need approval or when a genuinely disputed issue requires a judicial ruling.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **High Rate:** Vermont's top income tax bracket is 8.75% (32 V.S.A. § 5822), a comparatively high rate that makes distribution planning important.
- **UTC Adoption:** Vermont adopted the Uniform Trust Code in 2007 (Title 14A), giving trustees a consistent modern framework for duties, administration, and modification.
- **Expansion of Nexus:** Vermont's residency rule reaches trusts holding property transferred by Vermont residents or a Vermont-domiciled decedent's will.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would seriously impair the trust, following the Uniform Trust Code removal standard.
