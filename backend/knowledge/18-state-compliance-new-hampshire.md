# Trust Compliance: New Hampshire

## State Income Tax on Trusts

New Hampshire does not impose a state income tax on trusts. New Hampshire has no broad state income tax on individuals, estates, or trusts, making it one of the most tax-friendly jurisdictions in the country for trust administration. ## Filing Requirements

New Hampshire does not require the filing of a state fiduciary income tax return for trusts, as the state imposes no income tax on trusts or individuals. Trustees of New Hampshire trusts are not required to file a state trust tax return. However, trustees must still comply with federal filing requirements under IRS rules, including filing Form 1041 if the trust has taxable income of $600 or more. Trustees should also be aware of any federal information reporting requirements related to the trust's activities.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

New Hampshire does not have a statutory requirement for trustees to provide initial notice to beneficiaries upon accepting trusteeship or when a trust becomes irrevocable. The New Hampshire Trust Code (RSA 564-B) requires trustees to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed about the administration of the trust and the material facts necessary for them to protect their interests. Trustees must respond to beneficiary requests for information and provide reports. While there is no mandatory initial notice deadline, trustees should document all communications with beneficiaries to create a defensible record.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in New Hampshire is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. The New Hampshire Trust Code provides that a trust shall not be subject to continuing judicial supervision unless ordered by the court. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise or if judicial guidance is needed. The Circuit Court, Probate Division holds exclusive jurisdiction over RSA 564-B trusts.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **No State Income Tax:** New Hampshire's lack of a state income tax on trusts and individuals makes it a highly attractive trust situs. Trust income accumulates tax-free at the state level, benefiting both the trust and its beneficiaries.
- **Perpetual Trusts:** New Hampshire permits perpetual trusts with no maximum duration, making it an ideal jurisdiction for dynasty trust planning.
- **Directed Trusts and Trust Protectors:** New Hampshire's Trust Code provides robust support for directed trusts and trust protectors, allowing settlors to delegate investment and distribution decisions while maintaining oversight.
- **Spendthrift Protection:** New Hampshire allows self-settled asset protection trusts, providing grantors with creditor protection while retaining benefits from the trust.
- **Community Property:** New Hampshire is not a community property state.
