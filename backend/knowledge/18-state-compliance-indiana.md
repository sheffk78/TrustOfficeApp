# Trust Compliance: Indiana

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Indiana imposes a flat-rate income tax on fiduciary income. A trust's Indiana residence is determined by the place where it is administered: an Indiana resident trust is administered in Indiana, and a nonresident trust is administered outside the state. Resident trusts pay tax on all-source income; nonresident trusts pay only on Indiana-source income. Indiana's flat income-tax rate for fiduciary income has been declining in stages — 3.15% in recent years, dropping to about 3.05% and scheduled to decline further under the state's rate-reduction legislation (toward roughly 3.00%). Because the rate is flat, Indiana trusts do not use bracket management the way graduated-rate states do, but distribution planning still shifts tax burden to beneficiaries' own flat-rate liabilities.

## Filing Requirements

Indiana fiduciary income tax is reported on Form IT-41, the Indiana Fiduciary Income Tax Return, with estimated payments made on Form IT-41ES. The fiduciary representative is responsible for filing. Every estate or trust required to file must have a federal employer identification number (EIN). The filing deadline is the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year, aligned with federal Form 1041. Modernized e-file (MeF) is available, and paper returns remain an option. A trust's Indiana fiduciary return reports income accumulated or held for distribution.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Indiana has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, so it relies on its own trust statutes and the trust instrument for beneficiary information rights. The instrument generally governs the timing and content of beneficiary accountings and notices. Trustees should provide accountings on the schedule the instrument establishes, retain evidence of delivery, and follow any notice provisions for major trust events.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Indiana trusts is optional. Trusts are generally administered without routine court involvement and need not be registered with a court to be valid. Beneficiaries or trustees may petition for instructions, accountings, or trustee removal when disputes arise.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **No UTC Adoption:** Indiana has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, so legacy statutory and common-law trust rules apply. Trustees must rely on Indiana's trust statutes and the governing instrument.
- **Trustee Removal:** Indiana applies a "reasonable grounds" standard for trustee removal, giving beneficiaries a practical route to replace an underperforming trustee without proving an actual breach.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Indiana provides spendthrift protection by default, shielding trust interests from beneficiary creditors in most cases.
- **Flat Rate:** Indiana's flat fiduciary rate means the trust and its beneficiaries pay the same base rate, so timing distributions does not create bracket arbitrage the way it does in progressive states.
