# Trust Compliance: Rhode Island

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Rhode Island imposes a state personal income tax on trusts with Rhode Island nexus. A trust is generally subject to Rhode Island tax if it is a resident trust — meaning the grantor was a Rhode Island resident when the trust became irrevocable — or if it has Rhode Island-source income. Trusts are taxed using the state's personal income tax schedule, with the top bracket at 5.99%. Because Rhode Island taxes estates and trusts at the individual rates, undistributed trust income can reach the top bracket quickly, so trustee distribution planning matters.

Income distributed to beneficiaries is generally deducted at the trust level and taxed to the beneficiaries, mirroring federal conduit rules. Trustees should confirm estimated tax payment obligations, which apply when the trust expects to owe a sufficient amount of Rhode Island tax for the year, and make payments on the standard quarterly schedule.

## Filing Requirements

Rhode Island fiduciary income tax is filed on Form RI-1041, the fiduciary income tax return for estates and trusts, along with its instructions. The deadline for calendar-year trusts is April 15, or the 15th day of the fourth month after a fiscal year closes. Rhode Island generally follows federal filing thresholds, so an estate or trust that must file a federal Form 1041 will typically need to file a Rhode Island return if it has Rhode Island nexus. Extensions to file are available, but any tax due must be paid by the original deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Rhode Island has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code; instead, trust law is found in Title 18 of the Rhode Island General Laws ("Fiduciaries"). Rhode Island does not impose a separate statutory notice-of-trust registration or a pre-litigation notice mandate for the creation of a private trust. Notice obligations are driven primarily by the terms of the trust instrument and common-law fiduciary duties to reasonably inform beneficiaries of administration. Trustees should still maintain clear records of communications with beneficiaries, as disputes over adequacy of reporting are handled under general trust and fiduciary principles.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Rhode Island trusts is optional, and a trust need not be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate in an unsupervised manner. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the Superior Court for guidance or supervision if a dispute arises or if judicial direction is needed. Court supervision affords the trustee additional protection from liability but adds expense, so it is typically reserved for disputes, accountings that need approval, or requests for instructions.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **No UTC:** Rhode Island is one of the states that has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, so trustees operate under the General Laws and common law rather than a uniform statutory trust code.
- **Rate Structure:** Rhode Island uses a progressive personal income tax schedule topping out at 5.99% for estates and trusts.
- **Trustee Duties:** Fiduciaries owe common-law duties of loyalty and prudent administration, and are expected to account to beneficiaries periodically in accordance with the trust terms and general law.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would endanger the trust, following the general fiduciary standard reflected in the seed data.
