## Key Statutory Definitions

| What You Assume | What the Code Says | Citation |
|-----------------|--------------------|----------|
| **Trade or Business** — Any work you do for money | "The performance of the functions of a public office" | 26 USC §7701(a)(26) |
| **Employee** — Anyone who works for an employer | A narrowly defined statutory category under §3401(c), not the same as a common law employee | 26 USC §3401(c) |
| **Employer** — Anyone who pays someone to work | The person for whom an individual performs services as an "employee" under the statutory definition | 26 USC §3401(d) |
| **Wages** — All money you earn from a job | "All remuneration for services performed by an employee for his employer" — with 23 enumerated exceptions including agricultural labor, domestic service, ministerial services, and nonresident alien compensation | 26 USC §3401(a) |
| **Taxpayer** — Anyone who pays taxes | "Any person subject to any internal revenue tax" — a legal status, not a universal condition | 26 USC §7701(a)(14) |
| **U.S. Person** — Anyone in America | Specifically enumerated: U.S. citizens, residents, domestic partnerships, domestic corporations, domestic estates and trusts | 26 USC §7701(a)(30) |
| **Foreign Person** — Someone from another country | Any person or entity that is NOT a "U.S. person" under the statute — including Americans operating in a non-U.S. person capacity | 26 USC §7701(a)(30) |
| **Person** — A human being | Includes individuals, trusts, estates, partnerships, associations, companies, and corporations | 26 USC §7701(a)(1) |
| **Income** — All money you receive | Not defined in the IRC. The Supreme Court has held that income means "gain derived from capital, from labor, or from both combined" — not every receipt | Eisner v. Macomber, 252 U.S. 189 (1920) |
| **Withholding** — Money the government takes from your paycheck | A mechanism under §3402 that requires employers to deduct from "wages" as defined in §3401 — if remuneration is not "wages," no withholding obligation arises | 26 USC §3402(a) |

## Constitutional Tax Categories

| Type | What It Is | Constitutional Rule |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Direct Tax** | Capitation (per-person) tax or tax on property | Must be apportioned among states by population | Art. I, §9, Cl. 4 |
| **Indirect Tax / Excise** | Tax on privileged activities, not on property itself | No apportionment required | Art. I, §8, Cl. 1 |

**The key question:** If the income tax is an excise (the Supreme Court says it is), then it applies to the *activity* being taxed — not to all earnings regardless of source.

> "Taxation on income is in its nature an excise, entitled to be enforced as such." — Brushaber v. Union Pacific R. Co., 240 U.S. 1 (1916)

## The 23 Exceptions to "Wages" (§3401(a))

The code itself lists compensation that is **not** "wages" for withholding purposes:

1. Agricultural labor (unless covered under §3121(a))
2. Domestic service in a private home
3. Service not in the course of the employer's trade or business
4. Service performed by a minister in the exercise of ministry
5. Compensation of a duly ordained minister
6. Service performed by a member of a religious order
7. Service performed by a Christian Science practitioner
8. Service performed by a member of a recognized religious sect
9. Service performed for a school under §3121(b)(10)
10. Service performed by a student (full-time)
11. Service performed by a nonresident alien (as designated by regulations)
12. Service performed by an individual in a vessel or aircraft
13. Service performed by a temporary employee of a school or college
14. Service performed by an individual under 18 delivering newspapers
15. Tips under $20/month
16. Service performed by an employee of a foreign government
17. Service performed by an employee of an international organization
18. Service performed by a nonresident alien temporarily present in the U.S.
19. Service performed by a resident of a U.S. possession
20. Service performed by a student nurse
21. Service performed by an employee of a railroad retirement system
22. Service performed by an individual receiving supplemental unemployment compensation
23. Service performed outside the United States (for certain employers)

**Note:** This list is from §3401(a) itself. The code's own structure excludes more categories than most people realize.

## What "Trade or Business" Actually Means

The single most important definition in the course:

**26 USC §7701(a)(26):** "The term 'trade or business' includes the performance of the functions of a public office."

**What this means:** Under the code's own language, "trade or business" is not "your job" or "your company." It specifically references the performance of functions of a **public office** — a federal position.

**Why it matters:** Throughout the IRC, tax liability is repeatedly tied to activities conducted in connection with a "trade or business." If your private-sector work is not the performance of a public office function, the statutory chain that creates tax liability may not apply to you.

**The Supreme Court confirms this distinction:**
> "[E]arnings could only qualify as taxable (or as a measure of tax liability) if they are products of privileged activities." — Lost Horizons, summarizing Brushaber holdings

## Quick Self-Check

Before you accept any tax-related form, filing position, or advice at face value, ask:

1. **Does the code define this term the way I assume?** Check §7701 first.
2. **Is this remuneration "wages" under §3401?** Check the 23 exceptions.
3. **Am I a "taxpayer" under the code's definition?** Or am I assuming that status?
4. **Is my work the performance of a "trade or business"?** What does §7701(a)(26) actually say?
5. **If I sign this form, what status am I claiming?** Am I signing as a "U.S. person"? As a "taxpayer"?
