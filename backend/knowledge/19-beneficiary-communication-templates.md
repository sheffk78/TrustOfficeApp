# Beneficiary Communication Templates

## Why Written Communication Matters

Trustees owe beneficiaries a duty of information — the legal obligation to keep them reasonably informed about the administration of the trust and its financial condition. Every communication with a beneficiary should be documented in writing, because if a dispute ever arises, courts and examiners look first at the paper trail. A clear, dated record of what you told beneficiaries — and when — is one of your strongest defenses against claims of breach of trust. Verbal promises and unrecorded conversations leave you exposed; written communications create evidence that protects both you and the beneficiaries.

## Distribution Decision Letters

### Approval Letter Template

```
[Date]

[Beneficiary Name]
[Beneficiary Address]

Re: Distribution from the [Trust Name] ("Trust")

Dear [Beneficiary Name],

I am writing to inform you that your request for a distribution of $[amount] dated [request_date] has been approved. The distribution is being made pursuant to [distribution standard, e.g., "the health, education, maintenance, and support standard"] as set forth in Article [article_number] of the Trust Agreement.

The funds will be [transferred to your account / issued by check] on or before [date]. Please note that this distribution is reported on the Trust's income tax return and may have tax implications for you. I recommend you consult your tax advisor regarding the treatment of this distribution.

If you have any questions about this distribution, please do not hesitate to contact me.

Sincerely,

[Trustee Name], Trustee
[Contact Information]
```

### Denial Letter Template

```
[Date]

[Beneficiary Name]
[Beneficiary Address]

Re: Distribution Request from the [Trust Name] ("Trust") dated [request_date]

Dear [Beneficiary Name],

I have carefully reviewed your request for a distribution of $[amount] for the purpose of [stated_purpose]. After consideration, I have determined that this request cannot be approved at this time.

The Trust Agreement directs the Trustee to make distributions in accordance with [distribution standard, e.g., "the health, education, maintenance, and support standard"] as set forth in Article [article_number]. In exercising my discretion, I considered [list factors: current trust assets, other beneficiaries' needs, beneficiary's other resources, purpose of the request, etc.]. Based on these considerations, the requested distribution does not fall within the standard established by the Trust.

This decision was not made lightly. I am committed to administering the Trust in accordance with its terms and for the benefit of all beneficiaries. If your circumstances change or you wish to provide additional information, I am happy to reconsider the request.

Sincerely,

[Trustee Name], Trustee
[Contact Information]
```

## Annual Accounting Notice

```
[Date]

[Beneficiary Name]
[Beneficiary Address]

Re: [Trust Name] — Annual Accounting and Report

Dear [Beneficiary Name],

As required under [state trust code / Uniform Trust Code Section 813] and the terms of the Trust, I am providing you with the annual accounting for the [Trust Name] for the period of [start_date] through [end_date]. The enclosed report includes:

- A statement of trust assets and liabilities
- A record of all receipts and disbursements
- A summary of all distributions made
- Investment and asset valuations as of [end_date]

Please review the attached accounting. If you have any questions or wish to discuss the report, please contact me within [number] days of the date of this letter.

Sincerely,

[Trustee Name], Trustee
[Enclosure: Annual Accounting]
```

## Beneficiary Information Request

```
[Date]

[Beneficiary Name]
[Beneficiary Address]

Re: Your Request for Information — [Trust Name] ("Trust")

Dear [Beneficiary Name],

I have received your request dated [request_date] for information regarding the Trust. As Trustee, I am committed to keeping beneficiaries reasonably informed. In response to your request, I am providing the following:

- [Description of documents or information provided, e.g., copy of the Trust Agreement, recent annual accounting, list of trust assets]
- [Any information withheld and the legal basis for doing so]

If you require additional information, please submit your request in writing and I will respond within a reasonable time.

Sincerely,

[Trustee Name], Trustee
```

## Trustee Resignation Notice

```
[Date]

[Beneficiary Name]
[Beneficiary Address]

Re: Notice of Trustee Resignation — [Trust Name] ("Trust")

Dear [Beneficiary Name],

This letter serves as formal notice that I am resigning as Trustee of the [Trust Name], effective [resignation_date] or upon appointment and acceptance of a successor Trustee, whichever occurs [first / later], in accordance with Article [article_number] of the Trust Agreement and [applicable state trust code].

The process for appointing a successor Trustee is as follows: [describe process per trust document — e.g., named successor, beneficiary vote, court appointment]. I am working to ensure a smooth transition and will provide a full accounting of my administration through [accounting_date].

If you have questions about the transition, please contact me or [successor trustee name / legal counsel] at [contact information].

Sincerely,

[Trustee Name], Resigning Trustee
```

## Best Practices for Written Communications

- **Keep a professional, neutral tone.** Avoid emotional or defensive language. Every letter may be read by a judge, attorney, or examiner years later — write accordingly. State facts, cite the trust document, and explain your reasoning clearly.
- **Send communications promptly and on a schedule.** Respond to beneficiary requests within a reasonable time (generally 30–60 days). Deliver annual accountings on a consistent date each year. Delay breeds suspicion and can be construed as a breach of the duty to inform.
- **Retain every communication permanently.** Keep copies of all letters, emails, and responses in an organized, permanent record. Correspondence is part of the trust's books and records and must be preserved for the life of the trust and beyond, including after termination and discharge.
- **Include the legal basis; avoid unnecessary detail.** Reference the specific trust provision and distribution standard you applied. State your reasoning briefly. Do not include opinions about beneficiaries' character, speculation, or information about other beneficiaries' distributions — these create unnecessary risk.
