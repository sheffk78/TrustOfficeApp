# Crisis and Emergency Escalation

## When This Applies
This file applies when the trustee faces a crisis beyond normal administration — assets at immediate risk, legal deadlines pressing, or the trust under external threat. Acting on instinct can cause irreversible harm and personal liability. The guiding principle: stop, document, and contact a trust attorney before taking significant action.

## Suspected Fraud, Theft, or Unauthorized Transactions
- **Immediate steps:** Freeze suspect accounts, place holds with institutions, change credentials, block unauthorized signers. Do not confront anyone — that destroys evidence. Preserve every record.
- **Escalation:** Contact your trust attorney immediately. File a police report if theft is confirmed. Notify each institution in writing. If a co-trustee is involved, consult counsel first.
- **Documentation:** Keep a written timeline of every action — dates, times, who you spoke with. Do not alter or delete anything.

## Beneficiary Disputes and Litigation Threats
- **Do not engage in arguments** about distributions, trust terms, or administration. Make no promises, admissions, or off-the-cuff explanations.
- **Document everything:** Save every email, text, letter, and voicemail. Log dates and substance of calls. Accurate records are your best defense.
- **Contact your trust attorney** before responding to any formal demand, litigation threat, or petition to remove you.
- **Consider mediation:** If serious but not yet in court, ask whether mediation or a neutral-facilitated meeting could resolve it without litigation.

## Court Orders, Subpoenas, Audits, and Creditor Claims
- **Do not destroy, alter, or discard any records.** A court order, subpoena, or audit triggers a legal duty to preserve all relevant documents.
- **Contact your trust attorney immediately** and provide copies of everything received. Do not respond, produce records, or speak to claimants without counsel.
- **Do not make distributions** while a claim, lawsuit, or audit is pending unless your attorney approves. Paying prematurely can create liability.
- **Preserve all communications** and direct questions from beneficiaries, creditors, or third parties to your attorney.

## Trustee Incapacity, Death, or Sudden Inability to Serve
- **Successor trustee activation:** Locate the trust document and identify the named successor. Confirm whether a physician's certificate is required. Verify the successor's willingness.
- **Emergency interim measures:** Delegate authority only through a properly executed power of attorney or co-trustee arrangement approved by counsel. Never hand over access informally.
- **Court involvement:** If no successor is named or a dispute arises, a court may need to appoint a trustee or conservator. Have your attorney initiate it.
- **Notification of beneficiaries:** Inform beneficiaries once the successor is confirmed. Document every notification.

## Missing, Deceased, Incapacitated, or Exploited Beneficiaries
- **Due diligence:** Make documented, good-faith efforts to locate any missing beneficiary — address, email, phone, family contacts, public records. Keep a written record.
- **Guardian or conservator:** If a beneficiary is incapacitated, distributions may need to go to a court-appointed guardian. Confirm authority before paying.
- **Attorney involvement:** If a beneficiary is deceased, do not assume their share passes to their children or estate — the trust's terms and state law control. Contact your attorney.
- **Adult Protective Services:** If you suspect a beneficiary is being exploited, abused, or neglected, report to APS in the beneficiary's state. Do not confront the exploiter.

## Imminent Asset Threats (Foreclosure, Lapse, Damage)
- **Emergency protective actions:** If property faces foreclosure, a policy is lapsing, or an asset is damaged, take the minimum steps to preserve value — pay premiums, secure the property, bring the loan current — only if the trust has funds and the action is within your authority.
- **Insurance verification:** Confirm coverage is active. Photograph damage immediately. File claims promptly.
- **Professional contact:** Call your attorney and financial advisor before large payments or asset sales.
- **Documentation:** Record every protective action, call, and expense. The record of your response protects you later.

## Universal Crisis Rules
- **Preserve all records.** Do not delete, alter, or discard anything — statements, emails, texts, voicemails, or physical documents.
- **Do not move trust funds** without your attorney's explicit approval. Moving money in a crisis is a fast way to create liability.
- **Do not delete or alter communications** with beneficiaries, institutions, or professionals, even if uncomfortable.
- **Document every action** — date, time, what you did, who you contacted, why. A contemporaneous record is the strongest evidence of responsible trusteeship.
- **Contact professionals before acting.** Advice costs less than a mistake.
