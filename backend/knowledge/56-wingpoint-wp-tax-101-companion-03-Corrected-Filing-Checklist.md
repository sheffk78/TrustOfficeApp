# WP-TAX-101 — Corrected Filing Checklist

**WingPoint Course Companion | Version 1.0 | April 2026**

---

> If someone files an information return about you that mischaracterizes your receipts, you have a legal right to answer that testimony. This guide explains the forms and the process. It is education, not specific filing advice.

---

## The Principle

When someone files an information return (W-2, 1099, K-1) about you, that return is **sworn testimony**. It claims you received "income" subject to the tax.

If you do nothing, that testimony stands as fact. The law provides mechanisms for you to answer — not with silence, but with your own testimony.

**Silence is not resistance. Silence is acquiescence.**

The corrected filing process is the legal mechanism for answering incorrect testimony with your own.

---

## When a Correction May Be Appropriate

You might consider a corrected or supplemental filing when:

- Information on a W-2, 1099, or K-1 does not match your understanding of the code's characterization of your receipts
- You received an information return that characterizes your earnings as "wages" or "income" in a way that may not be accurate under the statutory definitions
- You previously filed a return that you now believe did not accurately reflect your understanding of the law
- You want to answer testimony filed about you with your own sworn testimony

## Form 4852 — Substitute for Form W-2

### What It Is

Form 4852 is titled **"Substitute for Form W-2, Wage and Tax Statement."** It is an IRS form specifically designed for situations where:

- You did not receive a W-2 from your employer, or
- The W-2 you received contains incorrect information

### What It Does

Form 4852 allows you to provide your **own testimony** about what was (or was not) received, replacing or correcting the employer's characterization on the W-2.

**Its existence proves something important:** The government recognizes that W-2s can contain erroneous characterizations, and it provides a specific form for you to correct them.

### General Process

1. **Obtain Form 4852** — Available from IRS.gov or by calling the IRS
2. **Complete the form** — Enter your understanding of the correct characterization of your receipts
3. **Attach it to your return** — File it with your 1040 (or equivalent return)
4. **Document everything** — Keep copies of all forms, correspondence, and records

### Key Points

- The form requires you to attempt to obtain the W-2 from your employer first
- You must explain how you determined the amounts on the form
- The form is filed under penalties of perjury — it is your sworn testimony
- The IRS may contact the employer to verify, but your testimony stands as your answer to theirs

## Correcting a W-2

### The Situation

Your employer issues a W-2 that characterizes your compensation as "wages" under §3401. If you believe that characterization may not be accurate under the statutory definition, you have the right to answer that testimony.

### The Forms

| Form | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Form 4852** | Substitute W-2 — provides your own characterization of your receipts |
| **Form 1040** | Individual income tax return — the vehicle for your testimony |
| **Form 1040X** | Amended return — corrects a previously filed return |

### General Sequence

1. **Review the W-2** — Understand what your employer has characterized and why
2. **Determine your understanding** — Based on the statutory definitions, what is your understanding of the correct characterization?
3. **Complete Form 4852** — Substitute your testimony for the employer's characterization
4. **File your return** — With Form 4852 attached, reflecting your understanding
5. **Document and retain** — Keep copies of everything, including the original W-2 and your Form 4852

### Important Notes

- The employer's W-2 and your Form 4852 may show different amounts — this is expected
- The IRS processes both documents — your testimony answers theirs
- Do not alter the original W-2 — file your own testimony separately
- Consistency matters — your characterization should be the same across all filings for a given year

## Correcting 1099s

### The Situation

Someone files a 1099 (1099-MISC, 1099-NEC, 1099-INT, etc.) that characterizes payments to you as reportable income. If you believe that characterization may not be accurate, the same principle applies: you have the right to answer that testimony.

### The Process

1. **Review the 1099** — Understand what has been characterized and by whom
2. **Determine your understanding** — Based on the statutory definitions, what is the correct characterization?
3. **File your return** — Include the 1099 amounts on the appropriate lines, but reflect your understanding of the correct characterization
4. **Document everything** — Keep copies of the 1099 and your return

### 1099-Specific Notes

- Different types of 1099s cover different types of payments — each has its own characterization
- The filer of the 1099 has their own obligations — your answer is your testimony, not theirs
- The principle is the same as with W-2s: answer the testimony, don't ignore it

## Correcting K-1s

### The Situation

A K-1 (Schedule K-1) reports your share of income, deductions, and credits from a partnership, S corporation, or trust. If the characterization on the K-1 does not match your understanding of the correct treatment, you have the right to address it.

### The Process

1. **Review the K-1** — Understand what has been reported and in what categories
2. **Understand the trust/partnership structure** — K-1s reflect the tax treatment chosen by the entity
3. **File your return** — Include the K-1 information but reflect your understanding of the correct characterization
4. **Document everything** — Keep copies of the K-1 and your return

### K-1-Specific Notes

- K-1s are generated by the entity's tax return — they reflect the entity's characterization, not necessarily yours
- Trust income reporting interacts with the trust's tax status (including 508(c)(1)(a) considerations)
- The trustee's understanding of the trust's status may affect the characterization on the K-1
- This is an area where the trust structure and the filing position intersect — coordination matters

## Supplemental Returns

### What They Are

A supplemental return provides additional information alongside a previously filed return. It does not replace the original — it adds to it.

### When They Are Used

- When you need to provide information that was not included on the original return
- When you need to clarify or expand on what was previously filed
- When you need to answer testimony that was filed after your original return

### The Form

Form 1040X is the standard form for amended/supplemental returns.

## The Transition Sequence

**This is not legal advice. This is an educational framework for understanding the process.**

### Principles for Any Transition

1. **Verify before you act.** Understand what the law says before you take any action. Read the code. Check the definitions. Verify your understanding.

2. **Document everything.** Keep copies of all information returns you receive, all forms you file, all correspondence. Documentation is your evidence trail.

3. **Consistency over speed.** It is better to be consistent and deliberate than fast and contradictory. The law rewards consistency. It does not reward haste.

4. **One step at a time.** You do not have to change everything overnight. Start with understanding. Then with answering testimony that has been filed about you. Each step builds on the one before it.

5. **Understand before you sign.** Every form you sign is testimony under oath. Not understanding what you are certifying is not a strategy — it is a risk.

### General Educational Sequence

| Step | Action | Purpose |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Read the statutory definitions (§7701, §3401) | Understand the terms before you use them |
| 2 | Review your W-2, 1099, K-1 against the definitions | See the gap between what is reported and what the code says |
| 3 | Understand Form 4852 and its purpose | Know the mechanism for substituting your testimony |
| 4 | Understand the W-4 and what each certification means | Make informed decisions about withholding |
| 5 | If you choose to file, document everything | Keep copies, track correspondence, maintain records |
| 6 | Seek qualified guidance for your situation | This course is education — individual circumstances require individual advice |

### What NOT to Do

- ❌ Do not file forms you do not understand
- ❌ Do not make claims you cannot support with the code
- ❌ Do not ignore information returns filed about you
- ❌ Do not file zero returns without understanding the legal basis
- ❌ Do not change your filing position without documenting your reasoning
- ❌ Do not assume that what works for someone else works for you

## Key Statutes Reference

| Statute | What It Does | Why It Matters |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| §3401(a) | Defines "wages" with 23 exceptions | Determines whether withholding applies |
| §3401(c) | Defines "employee" narrowly | Not everyone who works is an "employee" under the code |
| §3402(a) | Requires withholding on "wages" | Only applies to "wages" as defined in §3401 |
| §3402(f) | Establishes the W-4 mechanism | You provide the certificate — understand what you're certifying |
| §3402(n) | Exemption from withholding | Statutory provision for no withholding when no tax liability |
| §3402(d) | Employer not liable if employee pays | Safety valve in the system |
| §301.6402-3 | Return constitutes claim for refund | Your return is your claim |
| §7701(a)(26) | Defines "trade or business" | Tax liability is tied to this definition |
