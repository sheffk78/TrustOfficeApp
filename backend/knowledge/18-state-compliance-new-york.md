# Trust Compliance: New York

## State Income Tax on Trusts

New York imposes state income tax on trusts, making it one of the higher-tax jurisdictions for trust administration. A trust is subject to New York income tax if it is a resident trust — meaning the grantor was a New York domiciliary at the time the trust became irrevocable — or if it has New York-source income. Resident trusts are taxable on all income regardless of where it is sourced, while nonresident trusts are taxed only on New York-source income.

New York's income tax rates for trusts mirror individual tax rates, ranging from 4% to 10.9% depending on taxable income. Because the trust tax brackets are compressed, many trusts reach the top marginal rate at relatively low income levels. This makes income distribution planning especially important, as distributing income to beneficiaries in lower brackets can reduce the overall tax burden.

A critical New York rule: a resident trust is exempt from New York income tax if it has no New York-source income and no New York trustees. This "exempt resident trust" rule means that careful structuring — including using out-of-state trustees and avoiding New York situs assets — can eliminate New York tax exposure for otherwise resident trusts. However, this requires precise compliance and documentation.

## Filing Requirements

New York fiduciary income tax is filed using Form IT-205, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the fiscal year-end. If the trust files federal Form 1041 on extension, the New York filing is automatically extended, but payment of tax is still due by the original deadline.

Estimated tax payments are required if the trust expects to owe New York tax of $300 or more. Form IT-2105 is used for estimated payments, due quarterly on April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15. Nonresident trusts with New York-source income file Form IT-205 as well, reporting only New York-source income.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

New York does not have a single statutory beneficiary notice requirement identical to California's Probate Code 16061.7, but the New York Surrogate's Court Procedure Act and the Estates, Powers and Trusts Law (EPTL) impose trustee duties to account and inform beneficiaries. Under EPTL Section 7-2.4 and related provisions, trustees must provide accountings to beneficiaries, and beneficiaries have the right to request judicial settlement of accounts.

For trusts under court jurisdiction (typically testamentary trusts), the trustee must file annual accountings with the Surrogate's Court. For trusts not under court jurisdiction, the trustee should provide informal accountings to beneficiaries regularly. New York also requires that beneficiaries receive notice of certain trust events, including the right to receive a copy of the trust instrument upon request.

## Court Supervision

New York has a dual system: testamentary trusts (created by a will) are typically under the supervision of the Surrogate's Court and require periodic accountings. Revocable and irrevocable inter vivos trusts (created during life) are generally administered without court supervision. However, beneficiaries or trustees can petition the Surrogate's Court for judicial settlement of accounts, which provides a final and binding resolution.

Court-supervised trusts involve annual or periodic accountings filed with the court, notice to beneficiaries, and potential court approval of distributions. This adds cost but provides the trustee with protection from future challenges.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **High Income Tax:** New York's state income tax on trusts is among the highest in the nation, requiring careful tax planning.
- **Estate Tax Separate from Federal:** New York has its own estate tax with a different exemption amount than the federal estate tax exemption, requiring separate planning and filing.
- **Exempt Resident Trust Rule:** Resident trusts with no New York-source income and no New York trustees may be exempt from New York income tax.
- **EPTL Compliance:** The Estates, Powers and Trusts Law governs trust administration with detailed duties and accounting requirements.
