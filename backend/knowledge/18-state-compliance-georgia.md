# Trust Compliance: Georgia

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Georgia imposes a state income tax on trusts that are Georgia resident trusts or that have Georgia-source income. Resident trusts pay on income from all sources; nonresident trusts with Georgia-source income (such as real estate or business operations in Georgia) are taxed on that income. Georgia taxes fiduciary income under a flat-rate structure that has been ratcheting down in recent years — from 5.75% historically, down to 5.19%, and reduced again to 4.99% for tax years beginning January 1, 2026 under legislation signed in May 2026. A trust is generally resident if it is created by a Georgia resident or is administered in Georgia, so trustees should confirm residency before planning. Distributions deductible by the trust shift tax to beneficiaries.

## Filing Requirements

Georgia fiduciary income tax is reported on Form 501, the Georgia Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year, aligned with the federal Form 1041 schedule. Form 501 is filed electronically in most cases. Extensions are available using the associated 501X amendment process, and estimated payments may be required for larger liabilities. The Georgia return starts from federal income and applies Georgia modifications.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Georgia has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, and it operates under its own trust statutes. Georgia trust law imposes reporting duties on trustees: a trustee must keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed about the administration of the trust and of the trust's material facts needed to protect their interests, and beneficiaries are generally entitled to receive a copy of the trust terms and relevant trust information. Under the state's framework, notice of designated trust events must be provided within the applicable window (60 days in the state compliance model). Trustees should provide annual accountings consistent with the instrument, keep records of delivery, and follow the notice provisions for major trust events.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Georgia trusts is optional. Trusts are generally administered without routine court involvement and need not be registered with a court to be valid. Georgia courts supervise trusts only when a beneficiary or trustee petitions for instructions, accountings, or trustee removal, or when a dispute requires judicial resolution.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **No UTC Adoption:** Georgia has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, so legacy statutory and common-law trust rules apply. Trustees must rely on the Georgia trust code and the instrument.
- **Trustee Removal:** Georgia applies a "reasonable grounds" standard for trustee removal, giving beneficiaries a practical route to replace an underperforming trustee without proving a breach.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Georgia provides spendthrift protection by default, shielding trust interests from beneficiary creditors in most cases.
- **Falling Flat Rate:** Georgia's flat fiduciary rate has declined over recent tax years; trustees should confirm the current year's rate before making distribution and retention decisions.
