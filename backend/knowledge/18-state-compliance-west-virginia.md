# Trust Compliance: West Virginia

## State Income Tax on Trusts

West Virginia imposes a state income tax on trusts with West Virginia nexus. A trust is generally subject to tax if it is a resident trust — created by a West Virginia resident — or if it earns West Virginia-source income. West Virginia taxes estates and trusts using the same brackets applied to individuals. Note that the state cut income tax rates in 2026 (retroactive to January 1, 2026, under new legislation signed March 31, 2026): the 2026 rates span roughly 2.11% to about 4.58% for taxable income above $60,000, replacing the prior top rate in the 6.5% area. Trustees should use the current-year brackets each filing season.

Income distributed to beneficiaries is generally deductible at the trust level and taxable to the beneficiaries, following the federal conduit approach. Trustees should track estimated-tax obligations that apply when the trust expects to owe a threshold amount of West Virginia tax, and pay on the standard quarterly schedule.

## Filing Requirements

West Virginia fiduciary income tax is filed on Form IT-141, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return for Residents (with a corresponding form for nonresidents and part-year residents where needed). The deadline for calendar-year trusts is April 15, or the 15th day of the fourth month after a fiscal year closes. West Virginia generally requires a return for any estate or trust that must file a federal Form 1041 and has West Virginia nexus. Extensions to file are available, but any tax due must be paid by the original deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

West Virginia adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Article 9 of Chapter 44D of the West Virginia Code. Under the trust code, a trustee has a duty to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration and must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries when a trust becomes irrevocable or when a change of trustee occurs. West Virginia's duty-to-inform and notice provisions follow the Uniform Trust Code framework, so trustees should document notice delivery dates and maintain records of what information was provided.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of West Virginia trusts is optional, and a trust does not need to be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate in an unsupervised manner. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the circuit court for supervision or guidance when disputes arise or judicial direction is needed. Court supervision provides additional liability protection but adds cost and delay, so it is generally used only for accountings that need approval or when a genuinely disputed issue requires a judicial ruling.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **UTC Adoption:** West Virginia adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Chapter 44D (Article 9), giving trustees a consistent modern framework for duties, administration, and modification.
- **Rate Cuts:** The 2026 rate reduction lowered the top rate toward roughly 4.58% for taxable income above $60,000, retroactive to January 1, 2026.
- **Trust Code Compliance:** Trustees follow the Uniform Trust Code's duty-to-inform and accounting provisions, including responding to beneficiary requests for information.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would seriously impair the trust, following the Uniform Trust Code removal standard.
