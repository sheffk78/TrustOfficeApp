# Trust Compliance: New Jersey

## State Income Tax on Trusts

New Jersey imposes a state income tax on trusts with New Jersey nexus. Resident trusts and trusts with New Jersey-source income must file. Nonresident trusts with no New Jersey-source income generally have no filing obligation. New Jersey taxes trusts under its Gross Income Tax regime. The top fiduciary income tax rate is 10.75% for trusts with taxable income exceeding $1 million, among the highest in the nation. New Jersey allows a deduction for income distributed to beneficiaries, shifting the tax burden to beneficiaries in lower brackets. New Jersey also imposes a separate inheritance tax on certain beneficiaries, distinct from the income tax filing requirement.

## Filing Requirements

New Jersey fiduciary income tax is filed using Form NJ-1041, the New Jersey Gross Income Tax Fiduciary Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. The fiduciary of every resident estate or trust must file Form NJ-1041 if gross income, before exemptions or deductions, was more than $10,000 (prorated for part-year returns) during the tax year. An extension can be filed, which provides additional time to file but not to pay. Trustees must also provide each beneficiary with a Schedule NJK-1 listing the total income distributed and the New Jersey source income distributed.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

New Jersey does not have a statutory requirement for trustees to provide initial notice to beneficiaries upon accepting trusteeship or when a trust becomes irrevocable. The New Jersey Uniform Trust Code (N.J.S.A. 3B:31) requires trustees to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed about the administration of the trust and the material facts necessary for them to protect their interests. Trustees must respond to beneficiary requests for information and provide reports. While there is no mandatory initial notice deadline, trustees should document all communications with beneficiaries to create a defensible record.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in New Jersey is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise or if judicial guidance is needed. New Jersey courts can also intervene when a trustee requests instructions or when a beneficiary seeks enforcement of their rights under the trust. The New Jersey Superior Court, Chancery Division, has jurisdiction over trust matters.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Uniform Trust Code:** New Jersey adopted the UTC as N.J.S.A. 3B:31, effective July 17, 2016. The New Jersey Uniform Trust Code codifies trustee duties, powers, and beneficiary rights in a comprehensive framework.
- **Inheritance Tax:** New Jersey imposes a separate inheritance tax on transfers to certain beneficiaries. This is distinct from the income tax and applies based on the relationship of the beneficiary to the decedent.
- **Spendthrift Protection:** New Jersey allows self-settled asset protection trusts, meaning a grantor can place assets in a trust for their own benefit and shield those assets from creditors.
- **Perpetuity Period:** New Jersey abolished the rule against perpetuities in 1999, allowing trusts to last indefinitely for multi-generational wealth planning.
- **Community Property:** New Jersey is not a community property state, following the equitable distribution model for property division.
