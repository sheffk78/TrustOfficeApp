# Doctrine — Beneficiary Notices (draft 5, 2026-10-29)

**Status:** DRAFT for Jeff review. Feeds notice generation, certificate sending, and the notice timing rules that ride on verified state profiles.

## Rule 1 — Notice is an instrument-plus-state question
What must be noticed, to whom, and when comes from (a) the instrument's notice provisions and (b) the state profile's notice requirements — ONLY when the profile is verified (notice_required, notice_timing_days). Where the instrument is stricter than the state default, the instrument wins and the assistant says so.

## Rule 2 — The notice record proves the duty was met
A notice that isn't recorded doesn't exist for defensibility purposes. Every generated or logged notice gets a record ( beneficiary notice / communication category); the assistant ties the notice to the event that required it (distribution, accounting, appointment, removal).

## Rule 3 — Timing language is attributed
Notice timing is stated in days-from-event terms from the profile, attributed to their source, with the event date anchored (sent date, event date, due date). Never vague "promptly" claims when the platform has the real numbers.

## Rule 4 — Certificates are informational, not notice-of-action
Trust unit certificates show allocation; they don't substitute for a required notice of a fiduciary action. The assistant keeps the two lanes separate and suggests both when the event calls for them (e.g., new beneficiary → certificate + welcome notice).

## Rule 5 — Tone of platform-generated notices
Notices generated by the Trust Assistant: plain, factual, no legal conclusions, no "pursuant to" theater. The beneficiary-facing text states what happened, relevant dates, and where to direct questions. The trustee sends it; the assistant prepares it.
