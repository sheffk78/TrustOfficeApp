# Trust Compliance: South Carolina

## State Income Tax on Trusts

South Carolina imposes a state income tax on trusts with South Carolina nexus. A trust is generally subject to South Carolina tax if it is a resident trust — created by a South Carolina resident — or if it earns South Carolina-source income. South Carolina taxes estates and trusts using the same brackets applied to individuals, with rates ranging up to a top rate in the 6.2% area for the highest income levels. Because the top bracket is reached at a relatively modest level of taxable income, trusts with significant undistributed income can land in the upper brackets quickly.

Income distributed to beneficiaries is generally deductible by the trust and taxable to the beneficiaries, following the federal conduit approach. Trustees should track estimated-tax obligations, which apply when the trust expects to owe a threshold amount of South Carolina tax, and pay on the standard quarterly schedule.

## Filing Requirements

South Carolina fiduciary income tax is filed on Form SC1041, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The deadline for calendar-year trusts is April 15, corresponding to the federal deadline, or the 15th day of the fourth month after a fiscal year closes. South Carolina generally requires a return for any estate or trust that must file a federal Form 1041 and has South Carolina nexus or South Carolina-source income. Extensions to file are available, but tax due must be paid by the original deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

South Carolina has adopted parts of the Uniform Trust Code in Title 62, Chapter 7 of the South Carolina Code (the South Carolina Probate Code), and the state applies its trust code's notice and reporting principles. A trustee has a duty to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration, and must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries when a trust becomes irrevocable or when a change of trustee occurs. Trustees should document notice delivery dates, because the duty-to-inform provisions under the trust code create a defensibility burden for fiduciaries.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of South Carolina trusts is optional, and a trust does not need to be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate without ongoing court administration. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the Probate Court for supervision or guidance when disputes arise or judicial direction is needed. Court supervision offers liability protection but adds cost, so it is reserved for situations where an accounting approval or a judicial instruction is genuinely helpful.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Partial UTC Adoption:** South Carolina has adopted the trust provisions reflected in the state's probate code (Title 62, Chapter 7), so trustees work under a statutory trust-codes framework rather than pure common law.
- **Progressive Rates:** South Carolina applies its individual income tax brackets to estates and trusts, taxing undistributed trust income at rates up to the top bracket.
- **Prudent Investor Standard:** South Carolina generally follows a prudent investor standard for trust investment, and trustees owe ongoing duties of loyalty and prudent administration.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would endanger the trust estate, consistent with the statutory trust-code standard.
