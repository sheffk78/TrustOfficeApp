# Trust Compliance: Connecticut

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Connecticut imposes an income tax on trusts and estates under Connecticut tax law. A trust is a resident trust based on the situs of administration and the grantor's status rather than on where the fiduciary or beneficiary lives; nonresident trusts are taxed on Connecticut-source income. Connecticut conforms substantially to the Internal Revenue Code for fiduciary income, applying Connecticut modifications and its own rate structure. Income distributed to beneficiaries is generally deductible by the trust and taxable to the beneficiaries, so distribution planning matters. Connecticut's fiduciary rates are progressive, and higher-income trusts can reach the top bracket quickly, so trustees should plan distributions deliberately.

## Filing Requirements

Connecticut fiduciary income tax is reported on Form CT-1041, the Connecticut Income Tax Return for Trusts and Estates. Form CT-1041 can be filed electronically through the Department of Revenue Services' myconneCT portal. The filing deadline follows the federal schedule — the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year. Extensions are filed on Form CT-1041 EXT, and estimated payments are made on Form CT-1041ES. Trustees should also verify whether a Connecticut alternative minimum tax computation (Schedule I) applies.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Connecticut has adopted the Uniform Trust Code, and its framework defines trustee information and reporting duties to qualified beneficiaries rather than imposing a rigid event-keyed standing notice mandate. The trust instrument generally controls the timing and content of beneficiary reports. Trustees should provide annual accountings consistent with the instrument and retain evidence of what was delivered and when.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Connecticut trusts is optional under the UTC-based framework. Trusts are generally administered without routine court involvement and need not be registered with a court to be valid. Beneficiaries or trustees may petition for instructions, accountings, or removal when disputes arise, but day-to-day administration is unsupervised by default.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **UTC Status:** Connecticut adopted the Uniform Trust Code with effect from January 1, 2020, bringing modern, comprehensive trust administration rules to the state.
- **Flat vs. Progressive:** Connecticut taxes trust income under a progressive rate schedule, so bracket management and distribution timing can lower the combined trust-plus-beneficiary burden.
- **Trustee Removal:** Under the Connecticut framework a trustee may be removed for breach of trust, giving beneficiaries a standard route to address misconduct.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Connecticut provides spendthrift protection by default, shielding trust interests from beneficiary creditors in most circumstances.
