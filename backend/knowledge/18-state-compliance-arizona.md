# Trust Compliance: Arizona

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Arizona imposes a state income tax on trusts, making it a relevant tax jurisdiction for trust administration. A trust is subject to Arizona income tax if it is a resident trust or if it has Arizona-source income. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was an Arizona resident at the time the trust became irrevocable. For trusts created by will, the trust is a resident trust if the decedent was an Arizona resident at death.

Arizona taxes resident trusts on all income regardless of source. Nonresident trusts are taxed only on Arizona-source income, such as income from real property located in Arizona, business income from Arizona operations, or income from pass-through entities with Arizona operations. Arizona's income tax rates for trusts are relatively moderate compared to high-tax states, with rates ranging from 2.5% to 4.5% depending on taxable income, following the individual rate schedule.

Arizona does not have an exempt resident trust rule similar to New York or Illinois. A resident trust is taxable in Arizona regardless of whether the trustee is located outside Arizona, unless the trust has no Arizona-source income and specific conditions are met. Trustees should consult with Arizona tax counsel to determine whether out-of-state trustee arrangements reduce or eliminate Arizona tax exposure.

## Filing Requirements

Arizona fiduciary income tax is filed using Arizona Form 141, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, aligning with the federal Form 1041 deadline. If the federal return is extended, Arizona grants an automatic extension for filing, but payment of any tax owed is still due by the original April 15 deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

Estimated tax payments may be required if the trust expects to owe Arizona tax. Trustees should use Arizona Form 141ES for quarterly estimated payments, due on April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15. The threshold for estimated payments depends on the trust's expected tax liability, and trustees should monitor income throughout the year to ensure payments are adequate.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Arizona has beneficiary notice requirements under its trust code, which was significantly modernized in recent years. The Arizona Trust Code, codified in Title 14 of the Arizona Revised Statutes, requires trustees to keep beneficiaries reasonably informed about the trust and its administration. Under Arizona law, the trustee must provide beneficiaries with a copy of the trust instrument upon request and must provide regular accountings.

Arizona's modernized trust code includes specific provisions regarding beneficiary notice. The trustee must notify qualified beneficiaries of the trust's existence, the identity of the trustee, and the beneficiary's right to request a copy of the trust instrument and an accounting. This notice should be given within a reasonable time after the trustee accepts the trusteeship or the trust becomes irrevocable. Arizona's trust law modernization aimed to align the state with the Uniform Trust Code, providing clearer rules on trustee duties and beneficiary rights.

## Court Supervision

Arizona trusts are generally administered without court supervision. The Arizona Trust Code does not require trusts to be registered with the court to be valid or to operate. Trustees can administer trusts independently of court oversight, which is the standard practice. Court involvement is typically limited to disputes, requests for declaratory relief, or when a trustee or beneficiary petitions the court for guidance.

Arizona's trust law modernization, which updated the state's trust code, clarified the rules for unsupervised administration and provided additional tools for trustees to manage trusts efficiently without court involvement. This reduces administrative costs while maintaining protections for beneficiaries through the trustee's fiduciary duties.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Trust Law Modernization:** Arizona significantly modernized its trust code in recent years, aligning with the Uniform Trust Code and providing clearer rules on trustee duties, beneficiary rights, and trust administration.
- **State Income Tax:** Arizona imposes a moderate state income tax on resident trusts and nonresident trusts with Arizona-source income, requiring careful tax planning.
- **Community Property:** Arizona is a community property state, meaning trusts involving married couples must carefully track separate versus community property for tax and creditor purposes.
- **No Estate Tax:** Arizona does not impose a state estate tax or inheritance tax, simplifying post-death administration.
