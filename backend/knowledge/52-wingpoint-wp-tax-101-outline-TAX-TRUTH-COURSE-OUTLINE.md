## Course Purpose

An awakening course for WingPoint members who are just realizing that what they believed about taxes may not be what the law actually says. This course curates the clearest proofs from existing teachers and wraps them in lived experience from our own community.

**This is not a law degree. This is not legal advice.** This is a guided path through information that already exists — organized so a person waking up can follow it without drowning.

---

## Design Principles

1. **Awakening path, not a lecture.** Each module builds on the last. The reader should feel the ground shifting progressively, not get hit with a wall of statutes on day one.
2. **Community stories first, legal proof second.** Every module opens with a human moment before the law.
3. **Curate, don't reinvent.** Point to the existing teachers and their work. We organize the path — we don't need to prove it ourselves.
4. **Plain language.** Define every term. Short sentences. Questions, not declarations. Per BRAND-VOICE.md.
5. **No Duell.** Too advanced for this audience. If someone wants that depth, they'll find it on their own after this course gives them the foundation.
6. **Stay within WingPoint voice boundaries.** No forbidden language (sovereign citizen, beating the IRS, loophole, etc.). No specific tax result claims. "Within the law, not around it."
7. **Practical is mandatory.** Every theory module needs a "so what?" — a concrete action or question the reader can take away. No module ends in abstraction.

### Lesson 3: The Withholding Illusion

**Goal:** Show how the withholding system creates the *appearance* of obligation while the actual statutory definitions are narrow enough to exclude most private-sector compensation. This is where the "it's automatically taken from my paycheck" belief meets the law.

**Core Points:**
- §3401 — "wages" is defined narrowly, with 23 enumerated exceptions
- §3402 — withholding only applies to "wages" as defined in §3401
- §3402(d) — if the employee pays the tax, the employer is not liable even if withholding was required but not done
- The W-4 — what it actually is (a withholding allowance certificate), what it means to fill one out vs. not fill one out, and how to approach it informed
- How the system relies on voluntary compliance — you check the boxes, you claim the status, the system assumes you know what you're signing
- The CTC suppression story — if Hendrickson's book was wrong, why did the government work so hard to suppress it? (credibility proof, not conspiracy)

**Sources:**
- IRC Withholding Statutes (training: `02-withholding-employee/irc-withholding-statutes-20260424.md`)

### Lesson 4: Who the Law Actually Addresses

**Goal:** The most important module. Show that the definitions in the code don't mean what people assume they mean. The gap between common language and statutory language is where the truth lives.

**Core Points:**
- 26 USC 7701 — definitions that don't match everyday English
- "Trade or business" — what it means in statute vs. common usage
- "Employee" — the statutory definition and who it actually covers
- "Taxpayer" — a legal status, not a synonym for "person who earns money"
- The difference between a private citizen and a person engaged in a privileged activity
- State Citizen vs. Federal Citizen — two different legal statuses, two different tax obligations
- The State Citizens Service Center Manual — only federal citizens are subject to federal income tax; state citizens are not

### Lesson 7: Correcting the Record — W-2s, 1099s, K-1s, and Your Return

**Goal:** The most practical module. If the tax doesn't apply the way people assume, how do you correct what's already been filed? What do you do with a W-2 that mischaracterizes your income? How do you transition from your current filing position?

**Core Points:**
- **Corrected/supplemental returns** — what they are, when they're appropriate, how Lost Horizons teaches the process
- **Correcting a W-2** — Form 4852 (Substitute W-2), when and how to use it, what it means to dispute the characterization on a W-2 you received
- **Correcting 1099s** — the same principle applied to non-employee compensation reporting
- **Correcting K-1s** — how trust/partnership income reporting interacts with your filing position
- **The W-4 question** — what it means to fill out a W-4, what it means to not fill one out, what it means to correct one, and how to approach this informed
- **Transitioning your filing position** — the practical steps from where you are to where the law says you should be, without panic or overnight leaps
- The difference between correcting a return and filing a new one
- Why documentation and consistency matter more than speed
- **Important framing:** This module teaches *the process*, not *the outcome*. What each person does with this information is their own decision. WingPoint provides education, not specific filing advice.

### Lesson 8: Where the Trust Fits — 508(c)(1)(a) and Why It Matters

**Goal:** Bridge the gap between understanding the tax truth and knowing what to do about it. Show how a properly structured trust — particularly one organized as a 508(c)(1)(a) mandatory exception — operates within the framework the previous modules uncovered.

**Core Points:**
- What 508(c)(1)(a) actually says — the mandatory exception for churches and religious organizations
- How it differs from 501(c)(3): no application required, no IRS approval needed, automatic by law
- Why this matters in the context of what Modules 1-7 proved: if the tax applies to privileged federal activities, a trust organized under a mandatory exception operates outside that framework by design
- The trust is not a loophole — it's a structure that aligns with the legal distinctions already in the code
- Churches under §7611 have special audit protections — the IRS has limited authority to examine them
- How trustee governance keeps the trust on the right side of the line (inurement rules, purpose, operation)
- Why WingPoint teaches governance alongside structure — a trust without a trained trustee is a liability
- The revocation of election — how it connects to trust positioning (Freedom Law Group's process)
