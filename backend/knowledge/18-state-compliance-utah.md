# Trust Compliance: Utah

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Utah imposes a state income tax on trusts with Utah nexus. A trust is generally subject to Utah tax if it is a resident trust — created by a Utah resident — or if it earns Utah-source income. Utah uses a single flat income tax rate for all taxpayers, including estates and trusts. For 2024 the flat rate was 4.55%; for 2025 and later it is 4.5%. The same flat rate is applied to trust taxable income, so rate brackets are not a planning consideration in Utah the way they are in progressive states.

Income distributed to beneficiaries is generally deductible at the trust level and taxable to the beneficiaries, following the federal conduit approach. Trustees must make estimated payments when the trust expects to owe a threshold amount of Utah tax, and Utah administers these through its Taxpayer Access Point (TAP) portal.

## Filing Requirements

Utah fiduciary income tax is filed on Form TC-41 (Trust Tax), the fiduciary income tax return, along with its instructions. The filing deadline for calendar-year trusts is April 15, or the 15th day of the fourth month after a fiscal year closes. Utah generally requires a return for any estate or trust that must file a federal Form 1041 and has Utah nexus, per Utah Code Ann. § 59-10-2 and related provisions. Extensions to file are available, but any tax due must be paid by the original deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Utah adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Title 75, Chapter 7 of the Utah Code (the Utah Uniform Trust Code). Under the trust code, a trustee has a duty to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration and must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries when a trust becomes irrevocable or when a change of trustee occurs. When a trust becomes irrevocable, the trustee generally must provide notice within 30 days of the event to the qualified beneficiaries, identifying the trust, the trustee, and the beneficiary's right to request information and accountings. Trustees should document notice delivery dates carefully.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Utah trusts is optional, and a trust does not need to be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate in an unsupervised manner. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the District Court for supervision or guidance when disputes arise or judicial direction is needed. Court supervision provides additional liability protection but adds cost and delay, so it is generally used only for accountings that need approval or when a genuinely disputed issue requires a judicial ruling.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Flat Tax:** Utah applies a single flat income tax rate (4.55% for 2024, 4.5% for 2025 and later) to estates and trusts, simplifying rate planning.
- **UTC Adoption:** Utah adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Title 75, Chapter 7, providing a comprehensive modern framework for trust duties, administration, and modification.
- **30-Day Notice:** Upon a trust becoming irrevocable, notice to qualified beneficiaries is generally due within 30 days under the state's Uniform Trust Code provisions.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would seriously impair the trust, following the Uniform Trust Code removal standard.
