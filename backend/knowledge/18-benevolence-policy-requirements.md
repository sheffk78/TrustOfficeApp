# Benevolence Policy Requirements — IRS Compliance Reference

> **Purpose:** Knowledge file for the TrustOffice AI assistant and trustee education.
> Covers federal IRS requirements for written benevolence policies in charitable trusts.

---

## 1. What Is a Benevolence Policy?

A benevolence policy is a **written, board-approved document** that defines how a charitable trust or church organization distributes assistance to individuals in need. It serves as the governing framework for the benevolence program and is a key component of IRS compliance.

## 2. IRS Authority

- **IRC Section 501(c)(3):** Organizations must be organized and operated exclusively for charitable purposes. A benevolence policy demonstrates this operational commitment.
- **IRC Section 508:** Organizations (including churches) must notify the IRS of their existence and apply for tax-exempt status. For churches, this is automatic but documentation strengthens compliance.
- **Treasury Regulation §53.4946-1:** Governs private foundations' benevolence/distribution requirements.
- **IRS Publication 1828:** Tax Guide for Churches and Religious Organizations — outlines recordkeeping and reporting requirements.
- **IRS Publication 3833:** Disaster Relief — guidelines for benevolence during federally declared disasters under IRC Section 139.
- **Private Letter Ruling 201205011:** Clarified that benevolence programs must serve a "charitable class" (large or indefinite group) — not specific individuals.

## 3. Required Elements of a Written Benevolence Policy

### 3a. Charitable Class Definition
The policy must define **who** is eligible to receive assistance. This must be a **large or indefinite class** of individuals, not specific named persons.

**Examples:**
- "Indigent persons residing within 50 miles of the church"
- "Low-income families in the county experiencing temporary financial hardship"
- "Veterans facing housing instability"

**What is NOT a valid charitable class:**
- A single named individual
- A small, closed group (e.g., "the Smith family")
- Employees of the organization (employee assistance is compensation, not benevolence — see IRC §102)

### 3b. Eligibility Criteria
Specific, documented criteria that an applicant must meet. Must be:
- **Objective and verifiable** (not subjective judgment)
- **Consistently applied** to all applicants
- **Documented in writing** in the policy

**Common criteria:**
- Income thresholds (e.g., below 200% of federal poverty level)
- Geographic residency requirements
- Membership or attendance history (for church benevolence)
- Demonstrated financial need (with documentation)
- Crisis or emergency situation

### 3c. Types of Assistance Covered
The policy should specify **what types of needs** will be addressed:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Housing | Rent, mortgage payments, utility bills |
| Medical | Prescription costs, emergency medical bills |
| Food | Grocery assistance, meal programs |
| Transportation | Gas cards, emergency car repairs, bus passes |
| Education | School supplies, tuition assistance |
| Emergency | Crisis intervention, temporary shelter |
| Spiritual/Ministry | Ministry-related needs (only if church-related) |

**Important:** The policy can specify both **covered** and **excluded** types of assistance. Common exclusions:
- Credit card debt
- Business debts
- Legal fees
- Long-term ongoing support (vs. one-time crisis assistance)
- Non-essential purchases

### 3d. Per-Recipient Limits
The policy should define maximum assistance amounts:
- **Per-request limit** — maximum for any single disbursement
- **Per-recipient annual limit** — total assistance any one person can receive in a calendar year
- **Aggregate fund limits** — caps on total benevolence disbursements per period

**Example:** "No individual may receive more than $500 in benevolence assistance during any calendar year."

### 3e. Approval Process
Documented workflow for how decisions are made:
- **Decision authority:** Individual trustee, committee, or board vote
- **Approval thresholds:** Amounts below X = single approver; above X = full committee/board
- **Meeting requirements:** Approval must be documented in meeting minutes
- **Conflict of interest:** Recusal requirements for applicants who are related to board members

### 3f. Documentation Requirements
Recordkeeping standards for each benevolence action:
- **Application form** with applicant's financial need description
- **Supporting documentation** (invoices, bills, eviction notices, medical bills)
- **Committee meeting minutes** documenting each decision with rationale
- **Payment records** showing disbursement method and amount
- **Follow-up records** for repeat recipients

### 3g. Designated Gift Prohibition
**Critical IRS rule:** Benevolence funds **cannot** accept contributions earmarked for specific individuals.

- Donors must contribute to the **general benevolence fund**, not to assist a named person
- If a donor insists on designation, the contribution **cannot** be treated as a charitable deduction
- The policy must explicitly prohibit earmarked contributions
- All distributions must go through the committee approval process — no "named" distributions

### 3h. Employee Benevolence — Tax Treatment (IRC §102)
**General rule:** Benevolence payments to **employees** are taxable wages (IRC §102).

- Must be reported on Form W-2 as compensation
- Subject to income tax withholding and payroll taxes
- Cannot be excluded from income even if given through the benevolence fund

**Exception — IRC §139 (Disaster Relief):**
- During IRS-declared disasters, assistance to employees/family members may be tax-free
- Must be "reasonable and necessary" expenses related to the disaster
- Must not compensate for lost wages
- Requires documentation of the disaster declaration
- See IRS Publication 3833 for full requirements

## 4. Governance Best Practices

### Committee Structure
- **2-4 members** minimum for review committee
- Members should have **no personal interest** in specific applications
- At least one member should have **financial literacy** training
- Committee should meet **regularly** (at least monthly)
- **Confidentiality** must be maintained regarding applicants

### Record Retention
- All applications, supporting documents, and decisions: **retain for 7 years minimum**
- Meeting minutes: permanent retention recommended
- Financial records: follow state-specific retention requirements
- All records must be **organized and accessible** for IRS audit

### Reporting
- Annual reporting of total benevolence disbursements
- Breakdown by category of assistance
- Number of applicants served
- Average and median grant amounts
- Comparison to prior year

## 5. Common Compliance Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| No written policy | Loss of tax-exempt status | Create and adopt formal policy |
| Serving named individuals only | Private benefit / not charitable class | Define broad charitable class |
| Accepting designated gifts | Loss of donor deductibility + private benefit | Prohibit earmarked contributions |
| Employee benevolence without W-2 | Payroll tax penalties + IRS sanctions | Flag all employee recipients, issue W-2 |
| Inconsistent application | Allegations of discrimination | Document criteria, apply uniformly |
| Insufficient documentation | Cannot support deductions in audit | Require supporting docs for all disbursements |
| Commingling funds | Breach of donor trust + accounting issues | Separate restricted fund accounting |
| No board oversight | Loss of governance credibility | Committee review + board reporting |

## 6. State-Specific Considerations

Benevolence trust requirements vary by state. Key areas:
- **Registration requirements:** Some states require charitable solicitation registration
- **Annual reporting:** State charity registration offices may require separate filings
- **Trust law variations:** Some states have specific rules about charitable trust duration and enforcement
- **Attorney General oversight:** In many states, the AG has jurisdiction over charitable trusts
