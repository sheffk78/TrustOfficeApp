# Trust Compliance: Pennsylvania

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Pennsylvania imposes its personal income tax (PIT) on trusts with Pennsylvania nexus. A trust is generally taxable in Pennsylvania if it is a resident trust — created by a Pennsylvania resident — or if it earns Pennsylvania-source income. Pennsylvania uses a single flat personal income tax rate of 3.07% on taxable income, and this same rate applies to estates and trusts.

Unlike many states, Pennsylvania taxes only the types of income specifically enumerated in the state income tax statute — interest, dividends, rents, royalties, and certain other categories — rather than all federal taxable income. Distributions to beneficiaries are generally deductible to the trust to the extent they are required to be distributed, shifting the tax burden to the beneficiary. Trustees should carefully classify income because Pennsylvania treats some items differently from federal law.

Pennsylvania requires estimated tax payments for trusts that expect to owe PIT. Form REV-1630F is used to report underpayment of estimated tax by fiduciaries, and payments are due on the standard quarterly schedule (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15).

## Filing Requirements

Pennsylvania fiduciary income tax is filed on Form PA-41, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The deadline for calendar-year trusts is April 15, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of a fiscal year. Pennsylvania offers an automatic six-month extension to file using the PA-41 extension procedures, but an extension extends time to file only — the estimated/actual tax must be paid by the original due date to avoid penalties and interest.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Pennsylvania adopted the Uniform Trust Code in 2006, codified at 20 Pa.C.S. Chapter 77. Under the trust code, a trustee must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries when a trust becomes irrevocable or when the trustee is replaced, generally within a defined window after the event, and must keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration. Pennsylvania does not impose a separate pre-registration or statutory "notice of trust" filing with a court. In practice, the notice obligations are driven by the terms of the trust instrument and the Pennsylvania trust code's duty-to-inform provisions.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Pennsylvania trusts is optional, and most private trusts operate without ongoing court involvement. Trusts are not required to be registered with the Orphans' Court to be valid. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the court if a dispute arises, if judicial guidance on administration is needed, or where the trust instrument requires it. Court supervision provides protection from liability but adds cost and delay, so it is generally used only when disputes arise or directions are required.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Flat Rate:** Pennsylvania's flat 3.07% rate applies to estates and trusts, making rate planning less complex than in bracket-based states.
- **UTC Adoption:** Pennsylvania adopted the Uniform Trust Code in 2006 (20 Pa.C.S. Chapter 77), giving trustees a consistent modern framework for duties, administration, and modification.
- **Prudent Investor Rule:** Pennsylvania follows a prudent investor standard for trust investments under its fiduciary code, and trustees have ongoing duties to account to beneficiaries at least annually unless waived.
- **Removal Standard:** The statutory standard for trustee removal is broadly "breach of trust" or circumstances that endanger the trust estate, reflected in the Uniform Trust Code provisions.
