# Trust Compliance: Hawaii

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Hawaii imposes a state income tax on trusts that are resident in Hawaii or that have Hawaii-source income. Hawaii has adopted the Internal Revenue Code into its income tax law (Chapter 235, Hawaii Revised Statutes), with some modifications — notably Hawaii has not adopted the full federal bonus depreciation and Section 179 expensing provisions. Hawaii taxes income under a progressive rate schedule ranging from 1.4% to 11%, and trusts are taxed at the same graduated rates as individuals. Income distributed to beneficiaries is deductible by the trust and taxable to the beneficiaries, so distribution planning can meaningfully reduce the combined tax.

## Filing Requirements

Hawaii fiduciary income tax is reported on Form N-40, the Hawaii Fiduciary Income Tax Return. A trust must file if it has any taxable income, or if it has gross income of $400 or more subject to the Hawaii income tax law, regardless of taxable income. Nonresident estates and trusts file to report Hawaii-source income. The filing deadline is the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year, aligned with federal Form 1041. Extensions and estimated payments are available, and charitable remainder trusts must file a Hawaii Form N-40 showing no tax liability.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Hawaii has adopted the Uniform Trust Code, and its framework defines trustee information and reporting duties to qualified beneficiaries. Hawaii does not impose a rigid, event-keyed standing notice mandate beyond the UTC-based duties; the trust instrument generally controls the timing and content of annual beneficiary accountings. Trustees should provide accountings on the schedule the instrument establishes, keep records of delivery, and honor beneficiary requests for information.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Hawaii trusts is optional under the UTC-based framework. Trusts are generally administered without routine court involvement and need not be registered with a court to be valid. Beneficiaries or trustees may petition for instructions, accountings, or removal when disputes arise.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **UTC Status:** Hawaii adopted the Uniform Trust Code with effect from January 1, 2022, bringing modern, comprehensive trust administration rules to the state.
- **Trustee Removal:** Under the Hawaii framework a trustee may be removed for breach of trust, giving beneficiaries a standard route to address misconduct.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Hawaii provides spendthrift protection by default, shielding trust interests from beneficiary creditors in most circumstances.
- **Hawaii-Source Nexus:** Trusts that hold Hawaii real estate or business interests have filing and reporting obligations even when administered elsewhere, so trustees with Hawaii property must track nexus carefully.
