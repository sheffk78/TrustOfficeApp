# Trust Compliance: Idaho

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Idaho imposes a state income tax on trusts that are Idaho resident trusts or that have Idaho-source income. A trust is a resident trust if at least three of the following are present: the grantor is domiciled or resides in Idaho; the trustee is domiciled or resides in Idaho; the trust is governed by Idaho law; the trust property is in Idaho; or the trust is administered in Idaho. Estates and trusts generally pass income through to beneficiaries, who pay individual income tax, but a trust can also have its own Idaho taxable income and pay Idaho tax. Idaho does not distinguish between resident and nonresident for federal-trust income in some circumstances, and a foreign trust required to file a federal Form 1040NR files on Idaho Form 66.

## Filing Requirements

Idaho fiduciary income tax is reported on Form 66, the Idaho Fiduciary Income Tax Return. Filing is required when: a resident estate has gross income of $600 or more; a nonresident estate has Idaho-source gross income of $600 or more; a resident or nonresident trust has gross income of $100 or more and is required to file a federal return; or the trust must file federal Form 990-T for unrelated business taxable income with the applicable thresholds. The filing deadline is the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year. The trust or estate must furnish each beneficiary an Idaho Schedule ID K-1.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Idaho has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, and it relies on its own trust statutes and the trust instrument for beneficiary information rights. The instrument generally governs the timing and content of beneficiary accountings and notices. Under the state compliance model, notice of designated trust events must be provided within 30 days. Trustees should provide accountings on the schedule the instrument establishes and retain evidence of delivery.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Idaho trusts is optional. Trusts are generally administered without routine court involvement and need not be registered with a court to be valid. Beneficiaries or trustees may petition for instructions, accountings, or trustee removal when disputes arise.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **No UTC Adoption:** Idaho has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, so legacy statutory and common-law trust rules apply. Trustees must rely on Idaho's trust statutes and the governing instrument.
- **Trustee Removal:** Under the Idaho framework a trustee may be removed for breach of trust, giving beneficiaries a clear standard to address trustee misconduct.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Idaho provides spendthrift protection by default, shielding trust interests from beneficiary creditors in most circumstances.
- **Low Trust Filing Trigger:** Because a trust files at just $100 of gross income when a federal return is required, most active trusts have an Idaho filing obligation.
