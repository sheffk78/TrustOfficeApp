# Trust Compliance: Minnesota

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Minnesota imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Minnesota nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was a Minnesota resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Minnesota-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Minnesota-source income generally have no Minnesota income tax filing obligation.

Minnesota has a progressive income tax rate structure for trusts, with rates ranging from 5.35% to 9.85%. Fiduciary tax rates are the same as the rates for individuals who file as married filing separately. The Minnesota Department of Revenue applies these rates to the trust's Minnesota taxable income. An estate or trust with $600 or more of gross income assignable to Minnesota must file Form M2, the Income Tax Return for Estates and Trusts.

## Filing Requirements

Minnesota fiduciary income tax is filed using Form M2, the Income Tax Return for Estates and Trusts. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form M2 is due annually. The trustee of a trust or the personal representative of an estate is responsible for filing Form M2 and for paying the tax. Estimated tax payments are required if the trust expects to owe tax, with quarterly due dates.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Minnesota does not impose a mandatory statutory beneficiary notice requirement under the Minnesota Trust Code (Minn. Stat. Chapter 501C). Trustees are not required by statute to send a specific notice to beneficiaries upon acceptance, though the trust instrument may impose such obligations. However, Minn. Stat. Section 501C.0813 provides that if a settlor has prohibited a trustee from sharing information with beneficiaries, the trustee may seek judicial approval by filing a petition with the court. Beneficiaries are entitled to notice of any such petition under Section 501C.0203.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Minnesota is optional. A trust is not subject to continuing court supervision as a court-supervised trust except as provided in Section 501C.0205 or as otherwise ordered by the court (Minn. Stat. Section 501C.0201). If the district court assumes court supervision of the trust, all further court proceedings with respect to the trust shall be maintained under the district court's in rem jurisdiction. The Minnesota District Court (probate division) has subject-matter jurisdiction over proceedings concerning the internal affairs of trusts.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Asset Protection:** Minnesota allows spendthrift trusts under Minn. Stat. Section 501C.0502. A spendthrift provision restricts both voluntary and involuntary transfers of a beneficiary's interest.
- **Trustee Powers:** The Minnesota Trust Code provides trustees with broad powers, including the power to delegate duties, invest trust assets as a prudent investor, and make distributions according to the trust terms.
- **Court Approval for Information Withholding:** If the settlor has expressly prohibited the trustee from sharing information with beneficiaries, the trustee may seek judicial approval by filing a petition with the court.
