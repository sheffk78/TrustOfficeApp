# Trust Compliance: Massachusetts

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Massachusetts imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Massachusetts nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was a Massachusetts resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Massachusetts-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Massachusetts-source income generally have no Massachusetts income tax filing obligation.

Massachusetts has a flat income tax rate of 5.0% on trust income. Income received by trustees of trusts subject to the taxing jurisdiction of Massachusetts is subject to similar exemptions, deductions, and credits as though received by an individual subject to taxation in Massachusetts. The rates and rules are set forth under M.G.L. c. 62 and 830 CMR 62.10.1.

## Filing Requirements

Massachusetts fiduciary income tax is filed using Form 2, the Massachusetts Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form 2 is due annually. The trustee or personal representative must file if the trust has Massachusetts-source income or if the trust is a resident trust. Estimated tax payments may be required if the trust expects to owe a certain amount in tax for the year.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Massachusetts requires the trustee to notify qualified beneficiaries within 30 days after acceptance of the trust or the trust becomes irrevocable, whichever is later (M.G.L. c. 203E, Article 8, Section 813(b)). The notice must include the trustee's name and address and must be delivered or sent by ordinary first-class mail. This is a critical compliance requirement that trustees must not overlook.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Massachusetts is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the probate and family court department of the trial court for supervision if disputes arise. Court supervision can provide protection from liability for the trustee but adds cost and complexity.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Uniform Probate Code:** Massachusetts has adopted the Massachusetts Uniform Probate Code (M.G.L. c. 190B and c. 203E), which governs trusts, estates, and guardianships. The code provides detailed trustee duties and beneficiary rights.
- **Spendthrift Trusts:** Massachusetts allows spendthrift provisions under the Uniform Probate Code. A spendthrift provision restrains both voluntary and involuntary transfers of a beneficiary's interest.
- **Trustee Duties:** Massachusetts imposes detailed trustee duties under M.G.L. c. 203E, including the duty to act in good faith, in accordance with the trust terms and purposes, and in the interests of the beneficiaries.
