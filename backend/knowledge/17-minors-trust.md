# Minor's Trust (2503(c))

## What It Is

A Minor's Trust, also called a Section 2503(c) trust, is an irrevocable trust established under Internal Revenue Code Section 2503(c) that allows a donor to make a gift to a minor that qualifies for the annual gift tax exclusion. Normally, gifts in trust do not qualify for the annual exclusion because the beneficiary does not receive a present interest. The 2503(c) exception solves this: if the trust meets the requirements of the statute, the gift is treated as a present interest and qualifies for the annual gift tax exclusion. The trust must terminate when the beneficiary reaches age 21, at which point the remaining assets must be distributed to them outright or made available for a limited period.

## Key Governance Requirements

- **IRC Section 2503(c) compliance.** The trust must meet all four statutory requirements: (1) the beneficiary must be under 21 when the trust is created, (2) income and principal may be used for the beneficiary's benefit before 21, (3) any undisbursed assets must pass to the beneficiary at age 21 or be available to the beneficiary for a 60-day window after age 21, and (4) if the beneficiary dies before 21, the assets pass to their estate.
- **Age 21 termination.** The trust must give the beneficiary the right to withdraw all remaining assets at age 21. Some trusts distribute outright; others allow a 60-day withdrawal window. If the beneficiary does not withdraw, the trust can continue under different terms, but the initial 2503(c) qualification ends at 21.
- **No income restriction.** Unlike a 2503(b) trust (which requires all income be distributed annually), a 2503(c) trust has no income distribution requirement. The trustee can accumulate income or distribute it as needed for the minor's benefit.
- **Gift tax qualification.** Each contribution up to the annual exclusion amount (currently $18,000 per donor in 2024) qualifies for the annual gift tax exclusion. Married couples can jointly gift up to $36,000 per minor per year. A gift tax return (Form 709) is generally not required if the gift is under the annual exclusion, but the trust must be properly structured for the exclusion to apply.
- **Trustee discretion.** The trustee has discretion over distributions for the minor's health, education, maintenance, or support, but cannot be obligated to accumulate or distribute income in a way that defeats the present-interest requirement.
- **Irrevocable.** Once funded, the trust cannot be revoked by the donor. The assets belong to the trust for the benefit of the minor.

## Distribution Rules

- **Trustee discretion before age 21.** The trustee may distribute income and principal for the minor's benefit—typically for health, education, maintenance, and support—using prudent judgment. There is no mandatory distribution requirement.
- **Mandatory access at age 21.** At age 21, the beneficiary must have the right to receive all remaining trust assets, either outright or through a 60-day withdrawal window. This is non-negotiable for 2503(c) qualification.
- **Death before 21.** If the beneficiary dies before reaching 21, the trust assets pass to the beneficiary's estate, not back to the donor or the donor's family.
- **Post-21 continuation.** If the beneficiary does not withdraw within the permitted window, the trust can continue, but it no longer qualifies under 2503(c). Continued administration is governed by the trust's remaining terms and general trust law.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Missing the age-21 deadline.** If the trustee fails to give the beneficiary access to the assets at age 21, the 2503(c) qualification is lost retroactively, potentially triggering gift tax and penalties.
- **Treating it as a permanent trust.** A 2503(c) trust is a temporary vehicle. Trustees sometimes assume the assets stay in trust indefinitely, but the law requires age-21 access.
- **Unclear distribution standards.** The trust may say "for the beneficiary's benefit," but the trustee should apply a HEMS or ascertainable standard to avoid making distributions that look like gifts to the donor's family rather than support for the minor.
- **Failing to file Form 709 when required.** If contributions exceed the annual exclusion, a gift tax return is required. Overlooking this can produce penalties and IRS scrutiny years later.
