# Trust Compliance: Iowa

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Iowa imposes a state income tax on trusts that file or have Iowa nexus, reported under the state's fiduciary income tax rules. Iowa conforms substantially to the Internal Revenue Code for fiduciary income, applying Iowa modifications and the Iowa rate structure. A trust's situs for tax purposes is generally where the trust is administered; Iowa Administrative Code rules address situs for both testamentary and inter vivos trusts (a testamentary trust is taxable in Iowa if the decedent was an Iowa resident at death until the administering court's jurisdiction ends). Income distributed to beneficiaries is deductible by the trust and taxable to the beneficiaries.

## Filing Requirements

Iowa fiduciary income tax is reported on Form IA 1041, the Iowa Fiduciary Return. A return must be filed if the gross income of the estate or trust is $600 or more, regardless of the amount of tax due. Mandatory electronic filing applies for taxpayers with at least $250,000 in gross receipts, those claiming $25,000 or more in tax credits, or those required to issue at least 10 Schedule K-1s. The filing deadline is the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year. Estimated payments may be required for larger liabilities.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Iowa has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, so it relies on its own trust statutes and the trust instrument for beneficiary information rights. The instrument generally governs the timing and content of beneficiary accountings and notices. Trustees should provide annual accountings consistent with the instrument, retain evidence of delivery, and follow any notice provisions for major trust events.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Iowa trusts is optional. Trusts are generally administered without routine court involvement and need not be registered with a court to be valid. Beneficiaries or trustees may petition for instructions, accountings, or trustee removal when disputes arise, but routine administration is unsupervised.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **No UTC Adoption:** Iowa has not adopted the Uniform Trust Code, so legacy statutory and common-law trust rules apply. Trustees must rely on Iowa's trust statutes and the governing instrument.
- **Trustee Removal:** Iowa applies a "reasonable grounds" standard for trustee removal, giving beneficiaries a practical route to replace an underperforming trustee without proving an actual breach.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Iowa provides spendthrift protection by default, shielding trust interests from beneficiary creditors in most cases.
- **Filing Threshold:** Because Iowa requires a return at $600 gross income — a low bar — many modest trusts have a filing obligation even when no tax is due.
