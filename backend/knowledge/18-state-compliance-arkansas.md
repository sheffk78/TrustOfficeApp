# Trust Compliance: Arkansas

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Arkansas taxes fiduciary income under the Arkansas Trust Code framework referenced in the state's revenue statutes (Arkansas Code § 26-51-803). A fiduciary return reports the income of an estate or trust, and income is attributable to Arkansas when the trustee or personal representative is an Arkansas resident. Recent legislation (2025) moved to exempt from state income tax the income of non-grantor trusts administered by an Arkansas-resident trustee — meaning a trustee should determine whether the trust is a grantor trust under federal law, because grantor trusts remain subject to Arkansas income tax on trust income. Trust income that is attributable to Arkansas real property or business operations is generally taxable.

## Filing Requirements

Arkansas fiduciary returns are filed using Form AR1002F (sometimes referenced as AR1002 for estates and trusts). The filing deadline is the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year, consistent with the federal Form 1041 schedule. Trustees who are Arkansas residents report trust income, and nonresident trustees prorate income for Arkansas-situs property. Because Arkansas tax law changed recently on non-grantor trust exemptions, trustees should confirm current-year rules before filing. Estimated payments may be required for larger liabilities.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Arkansas has not enacted a formal statutory mandate requiring periodic notice to beneficiaries keyed to trust events, and its UTC-based trust code (Arkansas Trust Code) is adopted only partially. Beneficiary information and reporting rights generally flow from the trust instrument plus Arkansas common-law accounting duties. Trustees should provide annual accountings consistent with the instrument and maintain records of all beneficiary communications.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Arkansas trusts is optional. Trusts are generally administered without routine court oversight and need not be registered with a court to be valid. Beneficiaries or trustees can petition the court for instructions, accountings, or removal where disputes arise, but day-to-day administration remains outside the courtroom.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **UTC Status:** Arkansas adopted a modified version of the Uniform Trust Code in 2005, titled the Arkansas Trust Code (Arkansas Code Title 28, Chapter 73). Adoption is partial; trustees should confirm which UTC provisions apply to their particular trust.
- **Trustee Removal:** Under the Arkansas trust framework, a trustee may be removed for breach of trust, giving beneficiaries a clear route to address trustee misconduct.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Arkansas provides spendthrift protection by default, so a beneficiary's creditors generally cannot reach trust interests unless an exception applies.
- **Recent Tax Change:** Trustees must track whether their non-grantor trust qualifies for the Arkansas income-tax exemption to avoid overpaying.
