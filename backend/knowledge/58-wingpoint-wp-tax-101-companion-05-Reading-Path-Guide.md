# WP-TAX-101 — Reading Path Guide

**WingPoint Course Companion | Version 1.0 | April 2026**

---

> Take it in order. The sequence matters. Each source builds on the one before it. Skipping ahead creates confusion — not speed.

---

## How to Use This Guide

Each entry includes:
- **What it covers** — one-sentence summary
- **Difficulty** — foundational (start here) / intermediate / advanced (save for later)
- **Where to find it** — link or source
- **Read first** — the specific section to start with

---

## Level 1: Foundation

**Read these before anything else.** They establish the core framework that everything else builds on.

---

### 1. Lost Horizons — Nature of the Income Tax
- **What it covers:** The structural argument that the income tax is an excise on privileged activities, not a direct tax on all earnings
- **Difficulty:** Foundational
- **Where to find it:** losthorizons.com
- **Read first:** Bob's Bicycles analogy — read this before anything else. It makes the structural argument click in one sitting.

---

### 2. Lost Horizons — The 16th Amendment
- **What it covers:** What the Amendment actually changed (apportionment rule) and did not change (the nature of the tax as an excise)
- **Difficulty:** Foundational
- **Where to find it:** losthorizons.com
- **Read first:** The Brushaber holding section. The Supreme Court's own words are clearer than any summary.

---

### 3. IRS Publication 1828 — Tax Guide for Churches and Religious Organizations
- **What it covers:** The government's own description of automatic exemption, mandatory exceptions, and limited audit authority for churches
- **Difficulty:** Foundational
- **Where to find it:** IRS.gov (search "Publication 1828")
- **Read first:** The section on "Automatic Exemption" — this is the IRS saying churches don't need to apply for tax-exempt status. Read what they say about their own authority.

---

## Level 2: Intermediate

**Read these after the foundation.** They add depth, practical tools, and independent confirmation from different angles.

---

### 4. Cracking the Code — Pete Hendrickson
- **What it covers:** The detailed filing revelation — how the tax code actually works when you read what it says instead of what people assume it says
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Where to find it:** Book (previously suppressed, may require effort to locate)
- **Read first:** Chapters on statutory definitions and the "trade or business" analysis. Dense but essential for anyone who wants to understand the filing mechanics.

---

### 5. Freedom Law Group White Paper — Revocation of Election
- **What it covers:** The argument that 99% of Americans can legally revoke their election to be classified as taxpayers, reclassifying as "legal non-taxpayers" through a process codified by Congress
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Where to find it:** freedomlawgroup.us
- **Read first:** The "voluntary nature" section and the revocation process overview. The white paper provides a different angle that confirms the same conclusion.

---

### 6. SEDM — Know Your Customer (KYC) Disclosure
- **What it covers:** A practical legal notice for financial institutions — how to comply with banking requirements without disclosing SSNs or accepting U.S. person status when you're operating in a different capacity
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Where to find it:** sedm.org (Form 09.084)
- **Read first:** The "Purpose" and "Legal Foundation" sections. This is a practical tool you can use in real banking situations.

---

### 7. State Citizens Service Center Manual — Richard J. McDonald
- **What it covers:** The distinction between State Citizens (citizens of the several states) and federal citizens — only federal citizens are subject to federal income tax
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Where to find it:** Available through SEDM and sovereignty research channels
- **Read first:** The sections defining "State Citizen" vs. "United States citizen" and the case law supporting the distinction.

---

## Level 3: Advanced

**Save these for after the foundation is solid.** They provide deeper historical, philosophical, and monetary context. Jumping here prematurely causes confusion.

---

### 8. Modern Money Mechanics — Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago
- **What it covers:** How money is created from nothing through fractional reserve banking — the Fed's own publication
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Where to find it:** Federal Reserve archives, widely available online
- **Read first:** The "Money Creation" chapter. Short, clear, and written by the people who do it.

---

### 9. A Treatise on American Citizenship — John S. Wise (1906)
- **What it covers:** The origin, nature, and obligations of American citizenship — state vs. national, allegiance, and the structure of the two-citizen system
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Where to find it:** Public domain, available through Google Books and legal archives
- **Read first:** The chapters on "State Citizenship" and "National Citizenship." Wise's 1906 distinctions still matter.

---

### 10. SEDM — Corporatization and Privatization of Government
- **What it covers:** How government was transformed from a protector of private rights into a corporate franchise system that converts individuals into collectivist subjects
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Where to find it:** sedm.org (Form 05.024)
- **Read first:** The "Franchise" section. How government entities operate as corporations and what that means for private rights.

---

### 11. American Organic Law — The Four Foundational Documents
- **What it covers:** The Declaration of Independence, Articles of Confederation, Northwest Ordinance, and Constitution — how they establish the United States as a confederacy of sovereign states, not a unitary nation-state
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Where to find it:** Public domain, widely available
- **Read first:** The introduction establishing the relationship between the four documents. The framework changes how you read everything that follows.

---

### 12. Burlamaqui's Principles of Natural and Politic Law
- **What it covers:** The philosophical foundation the American Founders drew from — natural law, social contract, sovereignty, and the principles of civil government
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Where to find it:** Public domain (1751/1763 English edition), Google Books
- **Read first:** Book I on natural law. Dense but foundational for understanding the philosophical framework behind the Constitution.

## A Note on Pacing

**There is no deadline.** Some people read the foundational path in a weekend. Others take months. Both are fine.

**The wrong pace is the one that overwhelms you.** If you find yourself confused, go back one step. The foundation needs to be solid before you build on it.

**The community is here.** If you hit a wall, ask questions in the WingPoint community. Someone has been where you are.
