# Trust Compliance: Tennessee

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Tennessee does not impose a broad-based personal income tax on trusts. Historically Tennessee taxed only interest and dividend income through the Hall Tax, but that tax was repealed in phases and eliminated entirely beginning January 1, 2021. As a result, trusts with Tennessee nexus generally have no state income-tax filing obligation on investment-type income, which makes Tennessee an attractive trust-situs state from a tax perspective.

Tennessee does impose certain business and privilege taxes, but ordinary fiduciary investment income of a trust is not subject to a state income tax. There is no separate Tennessee fiduciary income tax return or state trust income tax. Trustees should still monitor for any tax due on certain entity income or gross-receipts-type income that might flow through a trust that owns a Tennessee business.

## Filing Requirements

Because Tennessee has no personal or fiduciary income tax, most trusts created by Tennessee residents have no Tennessee fiduciary income-tax return to file. There is no state equivalent of a federal Form 1041 for Tennessee. A trust may still need to file a Tennessee business or franchise tax return if it owns or operates a business in the state, so trustees with business-holding trusts should confirm those obligations separately.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Tennessee adopted the Uniform Trust Code in 2006, codified at Title 35, Chapter 15 of the Tennessee Code. Under the trust code, a trustee has a duty to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration and to respond to reasonable requests for information. When a trust becomes irrevocable or there is a change of trustee, the trustee generally must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries within a defined period. Tennessee's duty-to-inform and notice provisions follow the Uniform Trust Code framework, so trustees should document notice delivery dates carefully.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Tennessee trusts is optional, and a trust does not need to be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate in an unsupervised manner. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the Chancery Court for supervision or guidance when disputes arise or judicial direction is needed. Court supervision provides additional liability protection from the trustee but adds cost and delay, and is generally used only when an accounting approval or instruction is genuinely required.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **No Income Tax:** Tennessee has no personal income tax on trusts, and the Hall Tax was fully repealed effective January 1, 2021.
- **UTC Adoption:** Tennessee adopted the Uniform Trust Code in 2006 (Title 35, Chapter 15), giving trustees a consistent modern framework for duties, administration, and modification.
- **Trust Situs Benefits:** The absence of state income tax, coupled with a trust-friendly code, makes Tennessee a popular situs for dynasty and asset-protection-style trust planning.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would seriously impair the trust, following the Uniform Trust Code removal standard.
