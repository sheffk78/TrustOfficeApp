# Trust Compliance: Maryland

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Maryland imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Maryland nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was a Maryland resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Maryland-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Maryland-source income generally have no Maryland income tax filing obligation.

Maryland has a progressive income tax rate structure for trusts, with rates ranging from 2% to 6.50% for the 2025 tax year. The tax is calculated on Maryland taxable income, which is generally based on federal taxable income with Maryland-specific modifications. Fiduciaries must file Form 504, the Maryland Fiduciary Income Tax Return, to report and pay the tax.

## Filing Requirements

Maryland fiduciary income tax is filed using Form 504, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form 504 is due annually. Nonresident fiduciaries must also file Form 504NR. Estimated tax payments are required using Maryland Form 504D for first-time payers, with pre-printed vouchers for subsequent payments.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Maryland requires the trustee to notify qualified beneficiaries within 60 days after accepting a trusteeship (Md. Estates and Trusts Code, Title 14.5, Section 14.5-813(b)(1)). The notice must include the trustee's name, address, and telephone number. Additionally, within 90 days after the trustee acquires knowledge of the creation of an irrevocable trust, the trustee must notify qualified beneficiaries of the trust's existence and the identity of the settlor. This is a critical compliance requirement that trustees must not overlook.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Maryland is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the circuit court for supervision if disputes arise. Maryland courts with equity jurisdiction have broad authority over trust matters, including the power to compel accountings, order withheld distributions, and remove trustees.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Maryland Trust Act:** Maryland's trust law is codified in the Maryland Estates and Trusts Code, Title 14.5 (Maryland Trust Act). The Act follows the Uniform Trust Code structure and provides detailed trustee duties and beneficiary rights.
- **Spendthrift Trusts:** Maryland allows spendthrift provisions that restrain both voluntary and involuntary transfers of a beneficiary's interest. Discretionary trusts are protected from creditors of the beneficiary.
- **Trustee Removal:** Maryland courts have broad authority to remove trustees for breach of trust or other cause. Beneficiaries can petition the circuit court to remove a trustee entirely.
