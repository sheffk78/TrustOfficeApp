# Trust Compliance: Oregon

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Oregon imposes a state income tax on trusts with Oregon nexus. Resident trusts and trusts with Oregon-source income must file. Nonresident trusts with no Oregon-source income generally have no filing obligation. Oregon's graduated rates follow the individual income tax schedule, with the top rate on Oregon-source fiduciary income at 9.9%, among the highest in the nation. Oregon allows a deduction for income distributed to beneficiaries, shifting the tax burden to beneficiaries in lower brackets.

## Filing Requirements

Oregon fiduciary income tax is filed using Form OR-41, the Oregon Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form OR-41 is due annually. The fiduciary of a decedent's estate, trust, or bankruptcy estate uses Form OR-41 to report income, deductions, gains, losses, and any income tax liability. An extension can be filed, which provides additional time to file but not to pay. Oregon is tied to federal income tax filing, and the trust must include a copy of the federal Form 1041 when filing Form OR-41.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Oregon does not have a statutory requirement for trustees to provide initial notice to beneficiaries upon accepting trusteeship or when a trust becomes irrevocable. The Oregon Uniform Trust Code (ORS Chapter 130) requires trustees to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed about the administration of the trust and the material facts necessary for them to protect their interests. Trustees must respond to beneficiary requests for information and provide reports. Oregon's ORS 130.710 also requires trustees to furnish beneficiaries with a copy of the trust instrument upon request and to provide annual accountings, which is a heightened informational duty compared to many other UTC states.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Oregon is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise or if judicial guidance is needed. Oregon courts can also intervene when a trustee requests instructions or when a beneficiary seeks enforcement of their rights under the trust. ORS 130.050 provides that a trust is not subject to continuing judicial supervision unless ordered by a court.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Uniform Trust Code:** Oregon adopted the UTC effective January 1, 2006, as ORS 130.001-130.910, making it one of the earliest UTC adopters. Oregon's adoption has been in place for nearly two decades, creating a developed body of case law.
- **Electronic Trust Documents:** Oregon was among the first states to adopt provisions for electronic trust documents, providing flexibility in trust execution and administration.
- **Spendthrift Protection:** Oregon does not have a strong self-settled asset protection trust statute. Grantors should be aware that trusts created for their own benefit may not provide the same level of creditor protection as in states with dedicated asset protection trust laws.
- **Perpetuity Period:** Oregon has a 90-year perpetuities horizon, which falls short of the unlimited duration available in some other UTC states. Trusts cannot last forever under Oregon law.
- **Community Property:** Oregon is not a community property state, following the equitable distribution model for property division.
- **Elder Financial Exploitation:** Oregon has progressive statutory protections for vulnerable beneficiaries, including enhanced safeguards against financial exploitation.
