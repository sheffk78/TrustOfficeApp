# Trust Compliance: Virginia

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Virginia imposes a state income tax on trusts with Virginia nexus. A trust is generally subject to Virginia tax if it is a resident trust — created by a Virginia resident — or if it earns Virginia-source income. Virginia taxes estates and trusts using the same brackets applied to individuals, with rates ranging from 2% up to a top rate of 5.75% for higher-income taxpayers. Because the top bracket is reached at a relatively attainable level of taxable income, trusts with significant undistributed income can land in the upper brackets.

Income distributed to beneficiaries is generally deductible at the trust level and taxable to the beneficiaries, following the federal conduit approach. Trustees should track estimated-tax obligations, which apply when the trust expects to owe a threshold amount of Virginia tax, and pay on the standard quarterly schedule.

## Filing Requirements

Virginia fiduciary income tax is filed on Form 770, the Virginia Fiduciary Income Tax Return (with Form 770 instructions). The deadline for calendar-year trusts is May 1 — later than the federal April 15 deadline — or the 15th day of the fourth month for fiscal-year trusts in some cases; trustees should confirm the applicable deadline each year. Virginia generally requires a return for any estate or trust that has Virginia nexus or Virginia-source income and must file a federal Form 1041. Extensions to file are available, but any tax due must be paid by the original deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Virginia adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Title 64.2 of the Virginia Code (the Virginia Code trust provisions). Under the trust code, a trustee has a duty to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration and must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries when a trust becomes irrevocable or when a change of trustee occurs. Virginia's duty-to-inform provisions follow the Uniform Trust Code framework, so trustees should document notice delivery dates and maintain records of what information was provided.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Virginia trusts is optional, and a trust does not need to be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate in an unsupervised manner. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the Circuit Court for supervision or guidance when disputes arise or judicial direction is needed. Court supervision provides additional liability protection but adds cost and delay, so it is generally used only when an accounting approval or a genuine dispute requires a judicial ruling.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **UTC Adoption:** Virginia adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Title 64.2, giving trustees a consistent modern statutory framework for duties, administration, and modification.
- **Progressive Rates:** Virginia applies its individual brackets to estates and trusts, with a top rate of 5.75%.
- **May 1 Deadline:** Virginia's fiduciary return is due May 1 for calendar-year estates and trusts, a different deadline than most states and the federal return.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would seriously impair the trust, following the Uniform Trust Code removal standard.
