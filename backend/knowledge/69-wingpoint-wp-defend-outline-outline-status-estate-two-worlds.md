# Course: Standing Strong: Defending Your Trust and Your Position
**Detailed Lesson Outline — 8 Lessons**

---

## Lesson 1 — The Two Worlds: How Law Splits Reality in Two

**Goal:** Give the student a foundational map before any technical content lands. Everything else in this course builds on this frame.

**Key content to cover:**
- Open with a simple but striking idea: there are two parallel legal realities operating simultaneously — one for the living man/woman, governed by natural law, common law, and equity; and one for the "dead" legal fiction, governed by civil statutory codes, administrative process, and franchise law.
- Explain that most people are unknowingly operating exclusively in the second world while their birthright in the first world goes unclaimed.
- Distinguish the two worlds by jurisdiction: the living world operates in equity and substance; the dead world operates in statutory civil law, which is law written for government actors and the entities they create.
- Introduce the idea that *consent* is the bridge — you don't get pulled into the dead world by force; you are presumed to have consented through silence, participation, and failure to rebut.
- Cover the WingPoint framing of "private capacity" vs. public franchise: acting as a living man or woman vs. acting as surety for an artificial NAME.
- End with the central question the rest of the course will answer: *Which world are you currently operating in, and how do you change that?*

---

## Lesson 2 — What Birth Registration Actually Creates

**Goal:** Demystify birth registration — not as a conspiracy theory, but as a legal and equitable event with real consequences that can be understood and addressed.

**Key content to cover:**
- Walk through what actually happens at birth registration: a certificate is created, a NAME (in all-capital letters) is brought into existence, and the state records it as a legal event.
- Explain that the NAME is not you — it is a legal construct, a trust-like entity associated with your identity, but separate from the living man or woman.
- Cover the concept of the Certificate of Live Birth as the document evidencing that a trust-like structure was created with the state as administrator and you (without your knowledge) placed in a managed role within it.
- Introduce the idea that the state, as a result of registration, holds equitable interest in the estate associated with the NAME — and that interest was never lawfully claimed back.
- Reference the WingPoint framing: the trust documents distinguish between "acting in private capacity" as a "living man or woman" versus acting as "surety, representative, or accommodation party for any artificial PERSON."
- Important: Frame this practically and legally, not conspiratorially. The point isn't that the government is secretly evil — the point is that legal presumptions operate quietly until rebutted, and silence is interpreted as consent.

## Lesson 3 — The Decedent Estate vs. The Ancestral Estate

**Goal:** Introduce the two estate concepts that sit at the heart of this course's title — and make them concrete and understandable.

**Key content to cover:**
- Define a **decedent estate**: an estate administered as if the owner is dead or civilly deceased — which is the default status of the NAME in the statutory system. The NAME has no living claimant in the eyes of the system unless one appears and asserts their standing.
- Define an **ancestral (lineal) estate**: the private, living estate of the man or woman — the inheritance of rights, property, and standing that flows through bloodline and common law, not through statutory grant. The WingPoint Declaration of Trust explicitly references "the private ancestral lineal estate of the living man or woman" as distinct from the "public decedent estate."
- Explain how the system administers the decedent estate on your behalf through probate presumptions, government agencies, and franchise contracts — collecting from it, assigning duties to it, and directing its output — without your conscious participation.
- Introduce the practical consequence: when you sign documents, pay taxes, or interact with government agencies *as the NAME*, you are confirming the presumption that the NAME's estate is properly under administrative management — and that you are the manager of a public office, not the beneficiary of a private estate.
- Explain what it means to *claim* the ancestral estate: it is not a paperwork trick. It is a shift in standing, in how you hold yourself, and in what jurisdiction you consent to operate in.
- Use a simple analogy: imagine a family farm that was placed into a government program when you were born, and you've been paying to manage it under that program's rules your whole life — never knowing the farm was yours free and clear before it was enrolled.

## Lesson 4 — Cestui Que Vie: The Doctrine of the Presumed Dead

**Goal:** Introduce the historical and legal doctrine that underlies the entire status-correction framework — in a way that is accurate, grounded, and practically applicable.

**Key content to cover:**
- Introduce the **Cestui Que Vie Act of 1666** — the English statute passed after the Great Fire of London, when many people fled and could not be located. The Act allowed their estates to be administered as if they were dead (civilly deceased) if they could not be found within a set period.
- Explain how this doctrine was carried forward into common law and eventually into the American legal system — and how birth registration effectively re-triggers a version of this presumption: the living man/woman is "absent" from the legal record while only the NAME appears.
- Cover the three key elements: (1) the presumption of civil death or absence, (2) the administration of the estate on behalf of the presumed-dead, and (3) the right to "appear" and rebut the presumption by proving you are alive and present.
- Connect this directly to the WingPoint framework: the reason trust-centered architecture matters is that a trust operated by a living man in private capacity, held under common law and equity, is a mechanism for the living claimant to *appear* — to hold property and conduct affairs in a way that is clearly not the decedent estate.
- Discuss what "remedy" looks like under this doctrine: it is not a magic form or a single filing. It is a consistent pattern of operating from the correct status — private capacity, living man/woman, under natural law and equity — rather than as a statutory actor.
- Make clear that courts and agencies will not automatically honor this — the point is to build a clean, consistent record over time and to not re-volunteer into the dead world through careless contracting.

## Lesson 5 — Status: What It Is, Why It Controls Everything

**Goal:** Define "status" as a legal and equitable concept and show how it determines every interaction a person has with the system — from taxes to courts to banking.

**Key content to cover:**
- Define legal status plainly: your status is your position in a legal or equitable relationship — it determines what rules apply to you, what duties you carry, and what rights you can assert.
- Contrast the major status positions that are relevant to WingPoint students:
  - (a) U.S. Person / statutory citizen / taxpayer — voluntarily assumed through conduct and silence
  - (b) State national / American national — political membership without statutory franchise obligations
  - (c) Nonresident alien for tax purposes — the position of an American national domiciled in a constitutional state, not engaged in a "trade or business" within the federal statutory jurisdiction
- Explain how status is rarely assigned by force — it is *presumed* based on your conduct, filings, and failure to rebut. Every form you sign, every franchise you join, every benefit you accept is a statement of status.
- Cover the concept of domicile vs. nationality: your nationality is political (where you were born, who your people are); your domicile is a legal choice that determines which law governs you. Most people have accidentally domiciled themselves within statutory federal jurisdiction through their conduct.
- Introduce the idea that status correction is the *prerequisite* to everything else — including trust architecture. A trust operated by someone who is still acting in a statutory U.S. Person status will eventually be pulled back into that world. The trust is a tool for the living man; it only works as designed when the man is operating from the right status.
- Reference the WingPoint Declaration of Trust language: trustees act "solely in their private capacity... and not as surety, representative, or accommodation party for any artificial PERSON, office, or franchise."

## Lesson 6 — Nationality, Domicile, and the Right World to Live In

**Goal:** Give students a working understanding of the nationality/domicile distinction and what it means practically to live from the correct world.

**Key content to cover:**
- Expand on the nationality vs. domicile distinction introduced in Lesson 5: nationality is inherited and political — it cannot be taken without your consent; domicile is a legal construct that can be changed, and it has been quietly changed for most Americans through their voluntary participation in federal franchises.
- Explain what "constitutional states" are vs. federal territory: the 50 union states are foreign with respect to the federal government in many statutory contexts. An American national domiciled in a constitutional state is outside most federal statutory jurisdiction unless they voluntarily elect into it.
- Walk through how people accidentally elect into federal jurisdiction: Social Security enrollment, filing a Form 1040, signing W-4s, accepting federal benefits, registering a business under state statute — each of these is an act of consent that implies a particular status and domicile.
- Cover the concept of "clean hands in equity": equity will not aid a person who has been voluntarily operating in the public franchise world and then selectively tries to claim private status only when it's advantageous. Status correction requires consistency — you can't hold a Social Security card and a private trust and expect both to coexist without conflict.
- Introduce the practical path: not a dramatic renunciation or confrontation, but a quiet, deliberate process of *not re-volunteering* — letting elections expire, correcting withholding, building a consistent private record, and operating from the correct standing going forward.
- This lesson should feel like a sober, realistic conversation — not a "magic trick" lesson. Acknowledge that this is a long-term posture, not a one-time fix.

## Lesson 7 — Trust-Centered Architecture as a Status Tool

**Goal:** Connect the abstract doctrine of this course to the practical WingPoint tools students already have or are building — showing exactly why the private irrevocable trust is the right vehicle for operating from correct status.

**Key content to cover:**
- Explain why a private irrevocable ecclesiastical trust, created under common law right of contract and natural law, is structurally different from a statutory trust (grantor trust, revocable living trust, LLC, etc.) — and why that difference matters for status.
- The trust holds property in the private world: legal title is with the trustee acting in fiduciary capacity, not in the NAME; equitable title is with the beneficiary, who remains private.
- Trustees act as "living men and women in their private capacity" — not as officers of a public franchise — and this is written directly into the WingPoint trust indenture.
- Cover how the trust "appears" in the living world: it contracts, holds title, opens accounts, and conducts business — but does so as a private entity that traces its authority to contract law and natural law, not to any government charter or statutory grant.
- Explain the relationship between this course and the trustee training courses: *Governing as Trustee* and *Practical Execution* teach you *how* to operate the trust. This course teaches you *why* the trust is structured the way it is and what world it is designed to operate in.
- Address the "clean hands" requirement in practical terms: operating a private trust while continuing to act as a statutory person in daily life creates an internal contradiction. The trust is not a loophole — it is a reflection of a genuine status position. This lesson ties the doctrine back to the student's real life and their responsibility to live consistently from correct standing.

## Lesson 8 — Putting It Together: From Presumed Dead to Living Claimant

**Goal:** Synthesize the entire course into a clear, actionable framework and send the student forward with a settled understanding of where they stand and what to do next.

**Key content to cover:**
- Recap the arc: birth registration created a presumption → the NAME has been administered as a decedent estate → you have been living as a trustee/manager of a public franchise rather than as the beneficiary of your private ancestral estate → Cestui Que Vie provides the doctrinal basis for rebutting that presumption → status, domicile, and consistent private conduct are how that rebuttal is lived out → Trust-Centered Architecture is the practical tool for operating from the correct world.
- Walk through what "appearing as the living claimant" looks like day-to-day: it is not a one-time declaration. It is a consistent pattern — private contracts, private trusts, correct status on filings, not re-enrolling in franchises, operating from equity with clean hands.
- Introduce what comes next: this course is the *why*. The courses on trade, business, and taxes (Course 4) address the specific statutory constructs (wages, "trade or business," franchise income) that must be navigated. The Inner Work course (Course 6) addresses the internal obstacles — anger, victimhood, and the enemy construct — that prevent people from actually living from correct standing even after they understand it intellectually.
- Give the student a practical self-assessment: what elections have they made that conflict with private status? What steps are available to begin correcting them? What should they NOT do (confrontational filings, "sovereign citizen" tactics, reckless rejection of all obligations)?
- End with the WingPoint posture: this is not about rebellion or evasion. It is about knowing who you are, where you stand, and operating with integrity from that position — as a living man or woman, in private capacity, under the authority of natural law and the God who authored it.
