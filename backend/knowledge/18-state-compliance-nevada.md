# Trust Compliance: Nevada

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Nevada does not impose a state income tax on individuals or trusts. This is one of the most significant advantages for trusts domiciled or administered in Nevada, as trust income — including investment income, capital gains, and accumulated earnings — is not subject to state-level income taxation. Trustees of Nevada trusts are not required to file a state fiduciary income tax return.

The absence of state income tax makes Nevada particularly attractive for irrevocable trusts designed to accumulate and grow wealth over long periods. Since undistributed income is not eroded by state taxation, Nevada trusts can compound more efficiently than trusts in high-tax states. This is a core reason Nevada is a premier trust jurisdiction for wealth preservation and dynasty trust planning.

Trustees should note that if a Nevada trust holds assets or generates income sourced to another state, it may have filing obligations in that other state. For example, real estate in California or business income in New York could trigger nexus and tax in those states despite the trust's Nevada domicile. Trustees must evaluate all income sources for potential out-of-state obligations.

## Filing Requirements

Nevada does not require a state fiduciary income tax return because there is no state income tax. Trustees file IRS Form 1041 for federal income tax purposes as usual. Nevada has no estate tax or inheritance tax, so there are no state-level tax filings required upon the death of a grantor or beneficiary.

If the trust holds Nevada business entities, the trustee should confirm whether those entities have state-level obligations. Nevada has a Commerce Tax on certain businesses with Nevada gross revenue above $4,000,000, but this typically does not apply to passive investment trusts. Trustees should verify with a tax advisor whether any entity within the trust structure triggers a Nevada filing.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Nevada does not have a specific statutory beneficiary notice requirement triggered by a defined event (such as the trust becoming irrevocable or a change of trustee) in the same manner as California or Florida. However, the Nevada Uniform Trust Code, codified in Chapter 163 of the Nevada Revised Statutes, requires trustees to keep beneficiaries reasonably informed about the trust and its administration.

Under NRS 163, the trustee has a duty to provide beneficiaries with information about the trust, including a copy of the trust instrument upon request and regular accountings. The trustee must respond to a beneficiary's request for information within a reasonable time. While there is no mandatory 60-day notice requirement, trustees should proactively communicate with beneficiaries to fulfill fiduciary duties and reduce the risk of disputes.

## Court Supervision

Nevada trusts are generally administered without court supervision. Trusts are not required to be registered with a court to be valid or to operate. The Nevada Uniform Trust Code allows trustees to administer trusts independently of court oversight, which is the standard practice.

Nevada does offer a unique feature: the Nevada First Party Special Needs Trust and the Nevada self-settled asset protection trust can be established with or without court involvement. For self-settled asset protection trusts (NRS 166), the trustee administers the trust without ongoing court supervision, but the trust must meet specific statutory requirements to achieve creditor protection. The lack of required court supervision reduces administrative costs and maintains privacy.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Self-Settled Asset Protection Trusts:** Nevada is one of the leading jurisdictions for self-settled asset protection trusts under NRS 166, allowing a grantor to place assets in a trust for their own benefit while protecting those assets from future creditors after a two-year seasoning period.
- **365-Year Perpetuity Period:** Nevada has a 365-year perpetuity period for trusts, allowing dynasty trusts to last for centuries, far longer than the traditional rule against perpetuities.
- **No State Income Tax:** Nevada's absence of state income tax is a cornerstone of its appeal as a trust jurisdiction, maximizing wealth compounding.
- **Privacy Advantages:** Nevada trust administration offers strong privacy, with no requirement to file trust documents with the court or make them public record.
