# Trust Compliance: Colorado

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Colorado imposes a state income tax on trusts that are administered in Colorado or that have Colorado-source income. A resident trust is one administered in Colorado, and a nonresident trust is any trust not resident in the state. Colorado tax is imposed at a flat rate on modified federal taxable income. Every resident estate and resident trust with Colorado-source income must file a Colorado fiduciary income tax return if it is required to file a federal income tax return, or if it has a Colorado tax liability. Nonresident trusts with Colorado-source income pay tax apportioned in the ratio of Colorado taxable income to modified federal taxable income, using Schedule E of the fiduciary return.

## Filing Requirements

Colorado fiduciary income tax is reported on the Fiduciary Income Tax Return, Form DR 0105 (earlier form numbers vary by year). The deadline follows the federal schedule — the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year. Trustees must prepare the federal Form 1041 first, because the Colorado return starts from federal taxable income and applies Colorado modifications. Extensions and estimated payments are available and required for larger Colorado tax liabilities.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Colorado has adopted the Uniform Trust Code, and its framework defines when a trustee must provide information and reports to qualified beneficiaries. Colorado does not impose a rigid, event-keyed standing notice obligation beyond the instrument and UTC-based information duties; the trust instrument controls the timing and content of annual beneficiary reports. Trustees should send accountings on the frequency the instrument establishes and retain evidence of delivery.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Colorado trusts is optional, consistent with UTC principles. A trust does not need court registration to be valid, and most trusts are administered without ongoing court involvement. Beneficiaries and trustees may petition the court for instructions, accountings, or trustee removal when disputes arise.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **UTC Status:** Colorado adopted the Uniform Trust Code with effect from May 2, 2019. Its trust law is modern and UTC-based.
- **Flat Income Tax:** Colorado's flat-rate approach applies equally to trusts and individuals, so bracket planning matters less than in graduated states, but distributions still shift liability to beneficiaries.
- **Trustee Removal:** Colorado applies a "reasonable grounds" standard for trustee removal, giving beneficiaries latitude to replace a trustee who is not performing adequately, even without a proven breach.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Colorado provides spendthrift protection by default, protecting trust interests from beneficiary creditors in most cases.
