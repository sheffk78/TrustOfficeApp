# Trust Compliance: Louisiana

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Louisiana imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Louisiana nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the settlor was a Louisiana resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Louisiana-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Louisiana-source income generally have no Louisiana income tax filing obligation.

Louisiana has a flat fiduciary income tax rate of 3% on net taxable income for tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025. Louisiana taxable income of a resident estate or trust is computed as the amount of income taxed in accordance with federal law for the same taxable year, less the amount of the federal income tax deduction. The tax is imposed under La. R.S. 47:181 and La. R.S. 47:300.2.

## Filing Requirements

Louisiana fiduciary income tax is filed using Form IT-541, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form IT-541 is due annually. An automatic six-month extension of time to file is available under La. R.S. 47:103. Estimated tax payments can be made using Form R-541ES.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Louisiana requires the trustee to provide notice to beneficiaries, though the specific timing and content requirements differ from UTC states due to Louisiana's civil law system. Under the Louisiana Trust Code (La. R.S. 9:1721 et seq.), trustees owe beneficiaries duties of information and accounting, and beneficiaries have the right to receive an accounting upon termination or removal of the trustee. The seed profile indicates a 30-day notice requirement, but this timing should be verified against the specific trust instrument and Louisiana Trust Code provisions, as notice requirements in Louisiana are more dependent on the trust instrument and judicial practice than on a fixed statutory deadline.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Louisiana is available and can be more involved than in common-law states. Louisiana's civil law system provides for judicial oversight of trust administration, including proceedings for the appointment and removal of trustees. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise. Louisiana courts have broad authority over trust matters, and judicial supervision can provide protection from liability for the trustee but adds cost and complexity.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Civil Law System:** Louisiana is the only state that follows a civil law system rather than common law, a legacy of its French and Spanish colonial heritage. The Louisiana Trust Code (La. R.S. 9:1721 to 9:2252) governs all Louisiana trusts and differs significantly from the Uniform Trust Code.
- **Spendthrift Trusts:** Louisiana allows spendthrift provisions that restrain both voluntary and involuntary transfers of a beneficiary's interest. However, the protections and limitations differ from common-law states.
- **Trustee Compensation:** A trustee is entitled to reasonable compensation from the trust estate unless the trust instrument provides otherwise or the trustee waives compensation.
