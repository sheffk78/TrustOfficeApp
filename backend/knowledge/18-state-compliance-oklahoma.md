# Trust Compliance: Oklahoma

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Oklahoma imposes a state income tax on trusts with Oklahoma nexus. Resident trusts and trusts with Oklahoma-source income must file. Nonresident trusts with no Oklahoma-source income generally have no filing obligation. Oklahoma's fiduciary income tax rate is a flat 4.75% on the taxable income of trusts, relatively low compared to national averages. The top rate applies to all levels of trust taxable income. Oklahoma allows a deduction for income distributed to beneficiaries, shifting the tax burden to beneficiaries in lower brackets.

## Filing Requirements

Oklahoma fiduciary income tax is filed using Form 513, the Oklahoma Resident Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. A fiduciary must file Form 513 if the trust has a gross income of $600 or more during the taxable year, or any taxable income regardless of the amount. An extension can be filed, which provides additional time to file but not to pay. Nonresident trusts with Oklahoma-source income must also file Form 513.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Oklahoma has specific statutory notification requirements that go beyond the model UTC. Within 60 days of accepting a trusteeship, the trustee must notify qualified beneficiaries of the new trustee's name, address, and telephone number. ## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Oklahoma is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise or if judicial guidance is needed. Oklahoma courts can also intervene when a trustee requests instructions or when a beneficiary seeks enforcement of their rights under the trust.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Uniform Trust Code:** Oklahoma adopted the Oklahoma Uniform Trust Code (OUTC) effective November 1, 2025, as Title 60 O.S. ÃÂ§ÃÂ§175.1-175.57. The OUTC applies to all Oklahoma trusts regardless of date of formation, including existing trusts. This represents a significant modernization of Oklahoma's trust law framework.
- **Duty to Control Costs:** The OUTC expressly requires trustees to incur only costs that are reasonable in relation to the trust property, the purposes of the trust, and the skills of the trustee. This is a codified duty that ties cost control to the trustee's skill level and delegation decisions.
- **Spendthrift Protection:** Oklahoma allows self-settled asset protection trusts, meaning a grantor can place assets in a trust for their own benefit and shield those assets from creditors.
- **Perpetuity Period:** Oklahoma has abolished the rule against perpetuities, allowing trusts to last indefinitely for multi-generational wealth planning.
- **Community Property:** Oklahoma is not a community property state, following the equitable distribution model for property division.
- **Energy and Agricultural Assets:** Oklahoma's economy is heavily tied to oil, gas, and agricultural assets, and trusts holding mineral rights or working interests face unique administrative considerations.
