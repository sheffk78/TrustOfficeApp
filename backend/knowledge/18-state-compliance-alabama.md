# Trust Compliance: Alabama

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Alabama imposes a state income tax on trusts under Section 40-18-25 of the Code of Alabama. Resident estates and trusts pay tax on taxable net income from all sources; nonresident trusts pay on income with business situs in Alabama or from real property located in the state. For adjusted gross income of $1,500 or more, the fiduciary tax rate is 2% on the first $500 of taxable income, 4% on the next $2,500, and 5% on all income over $3,000, against an exemption credit of $1,500 on net income. Trustees should plan distributions carefully because Alabama allows a deduction for income distributed to beneficiaries, which can shift tax burden to beneficiaries in lower brackets.

## Filing Requirements

Alabama fiduciary income tax is reported on Form 41, the Alabama Fiduciary Income Tax Return. Resident trusts must file if they have net income over the filing threshold; nonresident trusts must file if they have Alabama-source income. The return is due on the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the tax year (April 15 for calendar-year trusts), mirroring the federal Form 1041 deadline. Extensions are available, and estimated payments may be required for larger liabilities. Alabama conforms closely to federal fiduciary income concepts, so a completed federal Form 1041 is the starting point.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Alabama has not adopted a formal statutory notice-to-beneficiaries requirement keyed to trust events, and the state trust code does not impose a standing periodic notice mandate. Beneficiary information rights generally derive from the trust instrument and Alabama's trustee accounting obligations. Trustees should follow any notice terms in the governing document and provide periodic accountings to beneficiaries on the schedule the instrument establishes, while keeping records of what was sent and when.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Alabama is optional. Trusts are generally administered without routine court involvement and do not need registration to be valid. Trustee removal, accounting questions, and disputes proceed through court only when someone petitions. Alabama's trust code provides the statutory frame for such proceedings, and a trustee or beneficiary can seek court guidance or an accounting review where needed.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **UTC Status:** Alabama adopted the Uniform Trust Code effective January 1, 2007, as Title 19, Chapter 3B of the Code of Alabama, but only partially — several UTC provisions were modified in the Alabama version. Trustees should confirm which specific UTC sections apply rather than assume full adoption.
- **Duty to Account:** Alabama holds trustees to a duty to report and account to beneficiaries, generally on an annual basis, consistent with the UTC framework; specific timing depends on the instrument and trust terms.
- **Spendthrift Default:** Alabama provides spendthrift protection by default, so a beneficiary's creditors generally cannot reach trust interests unless an exception applies.
- **Trustee Removal:** Alabama trustees may be removed for reasonable grounds, giving beneficiaries a mechanism for addressing trustee misconduct or incapacity.
