# Trust Compliance: North Dakota

## State Income Tax on Trusts

North Dakota imposes a state income tax on trusts with North Dakota nexus. Resident trusts and trusts with North Dakota-source income must file. Nonresident trusts with no North Dakota-source income generally have no filing obligation. North Dakota's graduated rates are notably low: 0% on the first $3,150, 1.95% between $3,150 and $11,325, and 2.50% above $11,325. With a top rate of just 2.5%, North Dakota is one of the most tax-friendly states for trust administration. North Dakota allows a deduction for income distributed to beneficiaries, shifting the tax burden to beneficiaries in lower brackets.

## Filing Requirements

North Dakota fiduciary income tax is filed using Form 38, the Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. A fiduciary for a resident trust or estate must file Form 38 if the fiduciary is required to file a federal Form 1041. A fiduciary for a nonresident trust or estate must file Form 38 if the fiduciary is required to file Form 1041 and the estate or trust received income from North Dakota sources during the year. An extension of time to file the federal Form 1041 automatically extends the time to file the state Form 38.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

North Dakota does not have a statutory requirement for trustees to provide initial notice to beneficiaries upon accepting trusteeship or when a trust becomes irrevocable. The North Dakota Uniform Trust Code (NDCC Chapters 59-09 through 59-19) requires trustees to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed about the administration of the trust and the material facts necessary for them to protect their interests. Trustees must respond to beneficiary requests for information and provide reports. While there is no mandatory initial notice deadline, trustees should document all communications with beneficiaries to create a defensible record.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in North Dakota is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, a trustee or beneficiary may petition the court for supervision if disputes arise or if judicial guidance is needed. North Dakota courts can also intervene when a trustee requests instructions or when a beneficiary seeks enforcement of their rights under the trust.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Uniform Trust Code:** North Dakota adopted the UTC in 2007, codified as NDCC Chapters 59-09 through 59-19, effective August 1, 2007. North Dakota's adoption is considered partial because it includes some state-specific modifications to the model UTC.
- **Spendthrift Protection:** North Dakota does not have a strong self-settled asset protection trust statute. Grantors should be aware that trusts created for their own benefit may not provide the same level of creditor protection as in states with dedicated asset protection trust laws.
- **Perpetuity Period:** North Dakota has not abolished the rule against perpetuities. Trusts are subject to the traditional common-law perpetuity period, which limits the duration of trusts.
- **Community Property:** North Dakota is not a community property state, following the equitable distribution model for property division.
