# WP-TAX-101 — W-4 Decision Guide

**WingPoint Course Companion | Version 1.0 | April 2026**

---

> The W-4 is not a contract. It is not a confession. It is a withholding allowance certificate — a form that tells your employer how much to withhold based on what you claim. Understanding what you are certifying is the difference between informed and uninformed.

---

## What Is a W-4?

The W-4 is officially called the **"Employee's Withholding Allowance Certificate."** It is created under the authority of **26 USC §3402(f)**.

Its purpose: to tell your employer how much federal income tax to withhold from your pay.

**What it is not:**
- It is not a contract
- It is not a declaration of tax liability
- It is not an admission that you owe tax
- It is not irreversible — you can file a new W-4 at any time

It is one thing: a mechanism for determining withholding amounts based on what you tell your employer about your expected tax situation.

---

## The Legal Framework

### §3402(a) — The Withholding Requirement

> "Except as otherwise provided in this section, every employer making payment of wages shall deduct and withhold upon such wages a tax determined in accordance with tables or computational procedures prescribed by the Secretary."

**Key point:** Withholding only applies to "wages" as defined in §3401. If the remuneration is not "wages" under the statutory definition, the withholding obligation does not arise.

### §3402(f) — The Withholding Allowance Certificate

On or before the date of the first payment of wages, the employer must request a withholding allowance certificate (W-4) from the employee. The employee furnishes the certificate.

**Key point:** You are asked to furnish this certificate. Understanding what each field means is the foundation of informed completion.

### §3402(n) — Exemption from Withholding

> "No withholding... shall be required... if the employee furnishes the employer with a certificate which certifies that the employee — (1) incurred no liability for income tax... for the preceding tax year, and (2) anticipates incurring no liability for income tax... for the current tax year."

**Key point:** The code itself provides a mechanism for claiming exemption from withholding. This is not a loophole — it is a statutory provision written into the law by Congress.

### §3402(d) — Employer Not Liable if Employee Pays

> "If the employer, in violation of the provisions of this chapter, fails to deduct and withhold the tax under this chapter, and thereafter the tax against which such tax may be credited is paid, the tax so required to be deducted and withheld shall not be collected from the employer."

**Key point:** Even if withholding was required but not done, the employer is not liable if the tax is ultimately paid. The system has built-in safety valves.

## What Each Field Means

**This guide explains what each field asks. What you do with this information is your decision.**

### Filing Status (Lines 1-3)

**What it asks:** Your tax filing status — single, married filing jointly, married filing separately, head of household.

**What you're certifying:** Your expected filing status for the tax year.

**What the code says:** §3402(f)(1)(A) requires the employee to furnish a certificate "which shall be in such form and contain such information as shall be prescribed by the Secretary."

**The question for you:** Does your situation match the status you are claiming? Are you certifying something you understand, or checking a box because HR told you to?

---

### Multiple Jobs or Working Spouse (Line 2a)

**What it asks:** Whether you (and your spouse, if filing jointly) have multiple jobs.

**What you're certifying:** That the withholding calculation should account for income from more than one source.

**The question for you:** Are you accurately representing your employment situation, or defaulting to the simplest option?

---

### Dependents (Line 3)

**What it asks:** Number of qualifying dependents and the corresponding tax credit amount.

**What you're certifying:** That you have dependents who qualify under the IRC's dependency rules.

**What the code says:** The dependency rules are specific — §152 defines who qualifies. Not everyone you support is a dependent under the code.

---

### Other Adjustments (Line 4)

**4(a) — Other income:** Income not from jobs that you want withholding to cover.
**4(b) — Deductions:** Itemized deductions exceeding the standard deduction.
**4(c) — Extra withholding:** Additional amount you want withheld per pay period.

**What you're certifying:** Additional income, deductions, or withholding preferences.

**The question for you:** Are you making informed choices about these adjustments, or leaving them blank out of habit?

---

### Exemption from Withholding (Line 5)

**What it asks:** Whether you claim exemption from withholding.

**What you're certifying (per the form itself):** "I claim exemption from withholding for 20XX, and I certify that I meet both of the following conditions for exemption: 1. Last year I had a right to a refund of ALL federal income tax withheld because I had NO tax liability, and 2. This year I expect a refund of ALL federal income tax withheld because I expect to have NO tax liability."

**What the code says:** §3402(n) provides the statutory basis for this exemption. It is not hidden. It is not a loophole. It is written into the law.

**The question for you:** Do you meet the conditions described? Have you verified your understanding of "no tax liability" under the code's definitions?

## Informed vs. Uninformed Completion

**Uninformed completion (what most people do):**
- Fill out the form the way HR tells you to
- Check "Single" or "Married" without thinking about it
- Claim the number of allowances someone suggests
- Never question what any of it means
- Sign at the bottom without reading the certification

**Informed completion (what this course enables):**
- Read each field and understand what you are certifying
- Check the definitions in the code — not the IRS's simplified instructions, the actual code
- Understand what "wages" means under §3401 before certifying that you receive them
- Understand what "employee" means under §3401(c) before certifying that you are one
- Know that §3402(n) exists and what it provides
- Make your own decision based on your understanding of what the law says

**The difference is not what you check — it is whether you understand what you are certifying when you check it.**

## The W-4 and Your Rights

**Due process rights that apply to tax testimony:**

1. **The right to testify.** Armstrong v. Manzo (1965): Due process requires "the opportunity to be heard at a meaningful time and in a meaningful manner."

2. **The right not to be bound by irrebuttable presumptions.** Heiner v. Donnan (1932): "A statute which imposes a tax upon an assumption of fact which the [presumed] taxpayer is forbidden to controvert is so arbitrary and unreasonable that it cannot stand."

3. **The right to correct testimony about you.** When someone files an information return about you (W-2, 1099, K-1), you have the right to answer that testimony with your own. The government provides the forms for this purpose.

4. **Protection from intimidation.** 18 USC §1512 makes it a crime to intimidate, threaten, or corruptly persuade someone from testifying in an official proceeding — including the filing of tax returns.

## Common Misunderstandings

**"I shouldn't file because filing admits I owe tax."**
→ Filing a return is testimony under oath about what you believe to be true. It does not, by itself, create liability. The content of the testimony matters, not the act of filing.

**"The W-4 is a trap."**
→ The W-4 is a form that asks you to state your withholding preferences. What makes it a trap is not understanding what you are certifying. An informed person who understands the definitions can complete the form accurately.

**"I can just not fill it out."**
→ You can, but your employer is required by §3402(f) to request it. If you do not provide one, the employer must withhold at the highest single rate. Not filling it out does not exempt you from withholding — it guarantees the maximum withholding.

**"Claiming exemption from withholding is illegal."**
→ §3402(n) explicitly provides for exemption from withholding when both conditions are met. It is a statutory provision, not an underground strategy.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. The W-4 is a withholding allowance certificate — a mechanism for determining withholding, not a confession of liability
2. Understanding §3401, §3402, and §3402(n) is the foundation for informed completion
3. The code provides specific mechanisms for exemption from withholding (§3402(n)) and for correcting testimony (Form 4852, corrected returns)
4. What matters is not whether you fill out the form, but whether you understand what you are certifying when you do
5. Each person's situation is different — this guide teaches what the form asks and what the code says. What you do with that information is your decision.
