# Trust Compliance: Wyoming

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Wyoming imposes no state income tax — there is no personal income tax, no corporate income tax, and therefore no fiduciary income tax on trusts. As a result, a trust created by a Wyoming resident, or a trust administered in Wyoming, has no Wyoming income-tax filing obligation on its ordinary investment income. This absence of state income tax makes Wyoming one of the most tax-favorable trust situs states in the country and a leading jurisdiction for dynasty trusts and long-term trust planning.

Because there is no income tax, there are no Wyoming estimated-tax payments, no Wyoming fiduciary income-tax return, and no Wyoming equivalent of a federal Form 1041. Trustees should still monitor for any separate taxes that might apply to a trust that owns or operates a Wyoming business or other income-producing enterprise.

## Filing Requirements

Wyoming has no fiduciary income-tax return for estates and trusts, because the state does not impose an income tax. A trust that owns a Wyoming business may have certain business, severance, or other taxes to address, and trustees should confirm those separately. Otherwise, the compliance burden for Wyoming trusts is much lighter on the tax side than in most states.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Wyoming adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Title 4, Chapter 10 of the Wyoming Statutes. Under the trust code, a trustee has a duty to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration and to respond to reasonable requests for information. When a trust becomes irrevocable or when a change of trustee occurs, the trustee generally must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries within a defined period. Wyoming's duty-to-inform and notice provisions follow the Uniform Trust Code framework, so trustees should document notice delivery dates carefully.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Wyoming trusts is optional, and a trust does not need to be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate in an unsupervised manner. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the District Court for supervision or guidance when disputes arise or judicial direction is needed. Court supervision provides additional liability protection but adds cost and delay, so it is generally used only for accountings that need approval or when a genuinely disputed issue requires a judicial ruling.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **No Income Tax:** Wyoming imposes no state income tax on individuals, corporations, or trusts, eliminating the fiduciary income-tax filing burden.
- **Situs Advantage:** Wyoming is a premier dynasty-trust and long-term-trust situs because of its no-income-tax status and flexible trust statutes (Title 4, Chapter 10).
- **UTC Adoption:** Wyoming adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Title 4, Chapter 10, giving trustees a consistent modern framework for duties, administration, and modification.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would seriously impair the trust, following the Uniform Trust Code removal standard.
