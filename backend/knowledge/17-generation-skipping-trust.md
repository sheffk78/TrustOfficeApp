# Generation-Skipping Transfer (GST) Trust

## What It Is

A Generation-Skipping Transfer trust, or GST trust, is a trust designed to transfer wealth to beneficiaries who are two or more generations younger than the grantor — typically grandchildren — while avoiding or minimizing the federal generation-skipping transfer tax. The GST tax is a flat tax imposed on transfers that "skip" a generation, set at the highest federal estate tax rate and applied in addition to any estate or gift tax. Each person receives a GST exemption that can be allocated to transfers during life or at death, shielding those transfers from the GST tax. For 2025 the GST exemption is approximately $13.99 million per person, meaning a married couple can shield nearly $28 million of skip-generation transfers. The GST trust is the primary vehicle for using that exemption to move wealth down to grandchildren (or further generations) without the tax that would otherwise apply at each generation level.

## Key Governance Requirements

- **GST exemption must be allocated to the trust.** The grantor can allocate the GST exemption either affirmatively on a timely filed gift tax return (Form 709) or rely on automatic allocation rules for certain direct skips and indirect skips. The trustee and grantor must understand which allocation method applies, because an unallocated exemption means the transfer is subject to the full GST tax.

- **Form 709 reporting for lifetime transfers.** Transfers to a GST trust during the grantor's lifetime must be reported on Form 709, and the GST exemption allocation is made on that return. Late allocations are permitted under the automatic allocation rules and relief provisions, but may result in different tax consequences depending on timing.

- **Understanding GST tax tiers.** The GST tax applies to three types of transfers: direct skips (a transfer to a skip-generation beneficiary outright or in trust), taxable distributions (distributions from a trust to a skip-generation beneficiary), and taxable terminations (a trust termination that causes a skip-generation beneficiary's interest to become possessory). The trustee must know which tier a given distribution triggers, because each has different reporting and tax implications.

- **Tracking exemption allocation and trust inclusion ratio.** Each GST trust has an inclusion ratio that reflects the portion of the trust not covered by allocated exemption. An inclusion ratio of zero means the trust is fully exempt and no GST tax applies to future distributions or termination. The trustee must maintain records of the allocation and the resulting inclusion ratio.

- **Proper identification of skip persons and beneficiary generation levels.** A "skip person" includes anyone assigned to a generation level two or more below the grantor, with specific rules for adopted children, deceased parents, and unrelated individuals. Misidentifying a beneficiary's generation assignment can lead to incorrect tax treatment.

## Distribution Rules

- **Direct skip distributions are taxed at the flat GST rate** to the extent the trust is not fully exempt (inclusion ratio greater than zero). The trustee is responsible for paying the tax or ensuring the beneficiary pays it.

- **Taxable distributions to skip persons trigger GST tax** on the amount distributed, reduced by any consideration received. The trustee must report these distributions and pay the tax from trust assets unless the trust instrument directs otherwise.

- **Exempt trusts (inclusion ratio of zero) permit distributions to skip-generation beneficiaries free of GST tax**, which is the entire point of allocating exemption to the trust at inception.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Failing to allocate the GST exemption.** Without an allocation on Form 709 (or reliance on automatic allocation where applicable), the trust assets are subject to the full GST tax at the highest estate tax rate. Late allocations are possible but produce a higher effective rate.

- **Misclassifying the type of GST transfer.** Confusing a direct skip with a taxable distribution or taxable termination leads to incorrect reporting on Form 709 and potential underpayment of tax, which carries interest and penalty exposure.

- **Assuming automatic allocation always applies.** Automatic allocation rules cover certain transfers but do not apply uniformly to all trust structures. A grantor who assumes automatic allocation without verifying the rules may leave a GST trust partially or fully unexempt.

- **Losing track of the inclusion ratio over time.** As trust assets grow or are distributed, the inclusion ratio determines the tax on each distribution. Trustees who do not maintain accurate allocation records cannot properly calculate GST tax and risk underreporting or overpaying.
