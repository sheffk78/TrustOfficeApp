# Trust Compliance: Michigan

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Michigan imposes a state income tax on trusts that have Michigan nexus. A trust is considered a resident trust if the grantor was a Michigan resident when the trust became irrevocable, or if the trust has Michigan-source income. Nonresident trusts with no Michigan-source income generally have no Michigan income tax filing obligation.

Michigan has a flat income tax rate of 4.25% for the 2025 tax year, applied to both individuals and trusts. The Michigan Department of Treasury uses Form MI-1041, the Michigan Fiduciary Income Tax Return, to tax the income an estate or trust earns while assets sit in fiduciary hands. Michigan taxable income is generally based on federal taxable income with Michigan-specific modifications.

## Filing Requirements

Michigan fiduciary income tax is filed using Form MI-1041, the Michigan Fiduciary Income Tax Return. The filing deadline is April 15 for calendar-year trusts, or the 15th day of the fourth month after the close of the fiscal year. Form MI-1041 is due annually. The fiduciary of a Michigan resident estate, a Michigan resident trust, or a nonresident estate or trust with Michigan-source income must file. Estimated tax payments are required if the trust expects to owe tax.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Michigan does not impose a mandatory statutory beneficiary notice requirement under the Michigan Trust Code (MCL 700.7100 et seq.). Trustees are not required by statute to send a specific notice to beneficiaries upon acceptance, though the trust instrument may impose such obligations. However, MCL 700.7813 requires the trustee to take reasonable steps to find trust assets and compel any person holding them to turn them over. Trustees should review the trust document carefully for any notice provisions.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of trusts in Michigan is optional. A trust does not need to be registered with the court to be valid. Trustees may operate trusts in an unsupervised manner, which is the default for most revocable and irrevocable trusts. However, the probate court has exclusive jurisdiction to appoint or remove a trustee (MCL 700.1302(b)(i)). A trustee or beneficiary may petition the probate court for supervision if disputes arise. Under MCL 700.7608, if no personal representative is appointed, the trustee of a revocable trust must publish and serve creditor notice, opening a four-month claims window.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **Asset Protection:** Michigan does not allow self-settled asset protection trusts. The Michigan Trust Code does not provide for DAPT (domestic asset protection trust) protections. A settlor who creates a self-settled irrevocable trust nonetheless allows the settlor's creditors access to the trust's assets.
- **Spendthrift Trusts:** Michigan allows spendthrift provisions under the Michigan Trust Code. A spendthrift provision restrains both voluntary and involuntary transfers of a beneficiary's interest.
- **Trustee Duties:** Michigan's Trust Code imposes detailed duties on trustees, including the duty of loyalty (MCL 700.7802), the duty of impartiality, and the duty to act in the interests of the beneficiaries.
