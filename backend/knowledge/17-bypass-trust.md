# Bypass Trust (Credit Shelter Trust)

## What It Is

A bypass trust, also called a credit shelter trust, is an estate planning trust funded at the first spouse's death with assets up to the applicable exemption amount, so that those assets pass free of federal estate tax and are sheltered from estate tax in the surviving spouse's estate as well. The trust "bypasses" the surviving spouse's taxable estate while still providing the spouse with access to income and, often, limited principal during their lifetime. Historically, when the estate tax exemption was much lower, the bypass trust was essential for married couples to use each spouse's exemption. Since 2011, portability allows the surviving spouse to use the deceased spouse's unused exemption without a bypass trust, but the credit shelter trust remains valuable for asset protection, controlling ultimate distribution, and sheltering appreciation from estate tax. It is commonly structured as part of an A-B trust arrangement, where the B trust is the credit shelter (bypass) trust and the A trust is the marital trust qualifying for the marital deduction.

## Key Governance Requirements

- **Funding at the first spouse's death must be done carefully.** The bypass trust is funded with assets equal to the applicable exemption amount (or a formula amount stated in the trust instrument) at the first spouse's death. Over-funding wastes the marital deduction; under-funding wastes the deceased spouse's exemption. The executor and trustee must coordinate the funding formula with the estate tax return.

- **The trust must not qualify for the marital deduction.** The entire purpose is to use the deceased spouse's exemption rather than defer tax through the marital deduction. Assets in the bypass trust are includible in the deceased spouse's estate but offset by the exemption, and they are not includible in the surviving spouse's estate.

- **Coordination with portability election.** Since 2011, a surviving spouse can elect portability on Form 706 to carry over the deceased spouse's unused exemption. If portability is elected and the bypass trust is not funded, the exemption is still usable by the surviving spouse — but the trust's asset protection and appreciation-sheltering benefits are lost. The executor must weigh whether to fund the trust, rely on portability, or do both.

- **Trustee must follow the terms governing the surviving spouse's access.** The trust instrument typically gives the surviving spouse rights to income and discretionary principal, often under an ascertainable standard (health, education, maintenance, support). Exceeding that standard can cause estate inclusion under IRC 2036 or 2038, defeating the bypass benefit.

- **Proper valuation of funded assets.** Assets transferred to the bypass trust at the first spouse's death must be valued as of the date of death (or the alternate valuation date). Accurate valuation is essential to confirm the funding equals the applicable exemption amount and to establish the trust's tax basis.

## Distribution Rules

- **Income typically goes to the surviving spouse.** Most bypass trusts require all income to be distributed to the surviving spouse at least annually, though the trust instrument can provide for accumulation or discretionary income distributions instead.

- **Principal distributions to the surviving spouse are discretionary under an ascertainable standard.** The trustee may distribute principal for the surviving spouse's health, education, maintenance, or support, but cannot distribute unlimited principal or to other beneficiaries during the spouse's lifetime.

- **On the surviving spouse's death, remaining principal passes to the ultimate beneficiaries** (often the couple's children) free of estate tax in the surviving spouse's estate, which is the core benefit of the structure.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Failing to fund the trust to the applicable exemption amount.** If the executor relies solely on portability and does not fund the bypass trust, the appreciation on those assets remains in the surviving spouse's taxable estate and is subject to estate tax at the second death.

- **Over-distributing principal to the surviving spouse.** Distributions beyond the ascertainable standard can cause the trust assets to be included in the surviving spouse's estate under IRC 2036 or 2038, entirely defeating the purpose of the bypass trust.

- **Missing the portability election on Form 706.** If the estate plan relies on portability instead of a bypass trust, the surviving spouse must elect portability on a timely filed Form 706. A missed election forfeits the deceased spouse's unused exemption.

- **Poor funding documentation.** Without a clear record of which assets funded the trust and at what values, the trustee cannot confirm the funding matched the applicable exemption amount, creating exposure to IRS adjustment and estate tax liability.
