# Trust Compliance: Wisconsin

## State Income Tax on Trusts

Wisconsin imposes a state income tax on trusts with Wisconsin nexus. A trust is generally subject to Wisconsin tax if it is a resident trust — created by a Wisconsin resident — or if it earns Wisconsin-source income. Wisconsin taxes estates and trusts using the same brackets applied to individuals, with rates ranging from 3.50% up to a top rate of 7.65% on higher taxable income. Because the top bracket is reached at a relatively attainable level, trusts with significant undistributed income can land in the upper brackets.

Income distributed to beneficiaries is generally deductible at the trust level and taxable to the beneficiaries, following the federal conduit approach. Trustees should track estimated-tax obligations that apply when the trust expects to owe a threshold amount of Wisconsin tax, and pay on the standard quarterly schedule.

## Filing Requirements

Wisconsin fiduciary income tax is filed on Form 2, the Wisconsin Fiduciary Income Tax Return, along with its instructions. The deadline for calendar-year estates and trusts is April 15, matching the federal deadline, or the 15th day of the fourth month after a fiscal year closes. Wisconsin generally requires a return for any estate or trust that must file a federal Form 1041 and has Wisconsin nexus or Wisconsin-source income. Extensions to file are available, but any tax due must be paid by the original deadline to avoid penalties and interest.

## Beneficiary Notice Requirements

Wisconsin adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Chapter 701 of the Wisconsin Statutes, effective for trusts in a modern statutory framework. Under the trust code, a trustee has a duty to keep qualified beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust's administration and must provide notice to qualified beneficiaries when a trust becomes irrevocable or when a change of trustee occurs. Wisconsin's notice and duty-to-inform provisions follow the Uniform Trust Code framework, so trustees should document notice delivery dates and maintain records of what was provided.

## Court Supervision

Court supervision of Wisconsin trusts is optional, and a trust does not need to be registered with a court to be valid. Most private trusts operate in an unsupervised manner. A trustee or beneficiary may petition the Circuit Court for supervision or guidance when disputes arise or judicial direction is needed. Court supervision provides additional liability protection but adds cost and delay, so it is generally used only for accountings that need approval or when a genuinely disputed issue requires a judicial ruling.

## Key State-Specific Rules

- **UTC Adoption:** Wisconsin adopted the Uniform Trust Code in Chapter 701 of the Wisconsin Statutes, giving trustees a consistent modern framework for duties, administration, and modification.
- **Progressive Rates:** Wisconsin applies its individual brackets to estates and trusts, with a top rate of 7.65%.
- **Prudent Investor Standard:** Wisconsin follows a prudent investor standard for trust investment, and trustees owe ongoing duties of loyalty and prudent administration under Chapter 701.
- **Removal Standard:** A trustee may be removed for breach of trust or where continued service would seriously impair the trust, following the Uniform Trust Code removal standard.
